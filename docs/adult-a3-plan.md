## 2. What is true today (read from the code on 2026-11-25; every line was grepped)

- `touch(save, kind, now)` is in `game/actions.ts` (the `TouchResult` interface at line 179, the function at 185). It returns `{ gained: 0, asleep: false }` when there is no living creature at home (`alive()`, line 36: the phase is `alive` and the creature has no `job`), `{ gained: 0, asleep: true }` for a creature that is asleep (night or nap), and `{ gained: 0, asleep: false }` for a poke, all before anything is paid. A pet or a tickle calls `noteCare`, then gives `tuning.affection.pet` (2) or `.tickle` (3) happiness, but at most `maxGainPerWindow` (15) per `windowMinutes` (60): `tuning.affection` in `data/tuning.ts` line 84, the window is `Creature.affection: { windowStart, gained }` (`state.ts` line 74, created in `sim.ts` line 102). The window resets when `now - windowStart >= 60 minutes` (lines 193 to 195), so after the clock is set back it does not reset until the clock passes the old end. `gained` is worked out before the clamp to 100, so a pet at 100 still uses the window and returns a positive `gained`.
- Petting in the picture (`ui/Stage.tsx`): a finger held on the creature for 450 ms starts petting, then one pet every 800 ms while held (lines 389 to 407). A plain tap is a poke: the creature startles and nothing is paid (`tap()`, line 295 on). Three taps inside 1,200 ms are a tickle (`touch(s, "tickle", t)`, the happy pose, four sparkles). A heart floats up only when `gained > 0`.
- The poses are `idle walk hop happy startle refuse wobble eat sleep rumble yawn`, the effects are `heart` and `sparkle` (`art/idle.ts` lines 17 to 30). No pose or effect is new in A3.1.
- `Creature.discipline` is 30 at hatching and nothing raises or lowers it; only combat reads it (the sulk chance, `data/combat.ts` line 32 and `game/combat/battle.ts` line 54: below 50, (50 - discipline) in a hundred, at most 30 percent, so a newborn's 30 sulks 20 percent of the time). The spec's "sulk never fires" (Spec 9) is wrong; what is true is that nothing raises discipline.
- A creature's optional fields (`job?`, `hp?`, `trained?`, `rung?`) are how a new field is added without a version bump: `Save.version` stays 3, and `migrateSave` (`state.ts`, line 216 on) coerces each. A creature is dropped with its save when it dies or retires, so a field on the creature starts again with the next egg.
- A creature can be `sleep: "night"` or `"nap"` (`state.ts` line 66; a nap ends by energy, `care.ts` line 51); `touch` treats both as asleep. A creature can be at home or in a field (`Creature.place`) and can be petted in both: the Stage refuses a pet only in the habitat and the arena (`Stage.tsx` line 397 on), and `touch` itself does not look at the place.
- Adult Mode is `settings.adultMode`, saved per profile; `isAdultMode(save)` is what the Stage already uses. Kid Mode stays as it is (CLAUDE.md section 12).
- Sync (`sync/sync.ts`): `noteSave(byPlayer)` marks a device changed only for a save made by a player action, never for a save that only carries the clock forward, and `planAfterPull` makes a device that is not marked adopt a newer server save. Petting goes through `game.act`, which is a player action.
- The Adult HUD (`ui/AdultHud.tsx`) is a strip (the creature's name and stage, the Book, Settings, the coin and material chips, the player) and a stat bar of five cells with a details card. Space is already short: decisions.md ("Space and size") measured the Adult room page at 360 x 640 as 83 px below the window at ratio 2 and 106 px at 320 x 568.

## 3. Decisions for A3 (mine, each changeable)

1. **Kid Mode is unchanged:** the hard cap of 15 an hour, no Bond, no new word. The taper and Bond are Adult Mode only (`isAdultMode(save)`), like the Shop. `touch` has one extra branch, not two systems that disagree: the spec's "do not keep both" is read as "the adult path replaces the cap", and the Kid path is the old rule until the owner says otherwise. Reversible: one condition.
2. **Bond belongs to the creature,** not the household: a new egg starts at level 1. Legacy (what a retired life leaves) is Spec 8, A4.
3. **Unlocks the HUD names are only the ones that exist.** The spec says every level shows its unlock; naming a trick that is two slices away would promise it. In A3.1 only L2 (a happy bounce of its own now and then, the existing `happy` pose with a heart; **corrected while building: the plan said an idle `hop`, but `chooseNext` in `art/idle.ts` already picks `hop` for about a quarter of an ordinary creature's choices, so a hop would not have been new**) and L5 (a bigger burst when petted, the existing effects: 3 more sparkles beside the heart a pet already gets (**corrected while building: the plan said "a bigger heart and sparkle burst"; there is no bigger heart**)) are real; each later slice adds its own (L3 and L7 tricks in A3.3, L4 the toy slot with the Shop's Toy Shelf, a later Shop slice, L10 the animation and the Book entry in A4). Levels with nothing to unlock still level up and say "Level 4". **Hat tolerance (the spec's L5 line) goes to A4,** with the arena: A2.4 left hats out of the arena and the hatch scenes on purpose (decisions.md, "Left out"), and "cosmetics stay on in-scene" means showing them there, which is combat art, not petting.
4. **XP is never tapered, only happiness is** (the spec: "always full Bond XP"). XP stops at the level 10 threshold so the number stays small.
5. **The streak** is the spec's "rhythm combo" read for the gesture the game really has. Only a held finger makes pets (one every 800 ms after 450 ms; a tap is a poke and three taps a tickle, and neither touches the chain). Pets one right after the other chain, and from the tenth pet in a chain (about 7 seconds of a held finger) each pet is worth 2 XP instead of 1. A gap of more than 2 seconds ends the chain. It changes XP only, never happiness.
6. **Poke stays free of reward and says so.** The spec's "wakes a dozing (not sleeping) creature" would be a nap, and I do not wake a nap with a poke: a nap ends by energy (`care.ts`), a poke waking it would add a rule to the care code and a way to cut a creature's rest short, and a napping creature is asleep for `touch` as it is today (no XP, no chain). "Starts a play invite" is A3.3. In A3.1 poke only gets its honest label in Adult Mode: "Poke: gets attention". Reversible: one more branch in `tap()`.
7. **The Bond display is placed after a measurement, not by a guess** (done, section 9: a chip in the bag row that opens a card, as (a) with (c)'s content on demand). Three candidates, in my order of preference: (a) one chip in the strip beside the bag chips, (b) a thin row under the stat bar, (c) a line in the stat details card. A quick mock at 320, 360 and 390 at pixel ratios 1, 2 and 3 (as the Home's H0 did) gives the real heights and widths before any code; the plan's figures are not summed.

## 4. A3.1 in detail

### The rules

- **Levels:** XP at which each level starts, `[tune]`: L2 300, L3 800, L4 1,500, L5 3,000, L6 4,500, L7 6,500, L8 9,000, L9 12,000, L10 15,000. Worked out for the spec's targets from 5 minutes of petting a day (300 s / 0.8 s = 375 pets, at most 9 + 366 x 2 = 741 XP): L2 in under a day, L5 in about 4 days and L10 in about 20 days of that, which real play (lifting the finger ends a chain, and not every day) stretches toward the spec's week and month. The later slices add XP sources, so these are retuned then.
- **XP per pet:** 1 while the chain is under 10, 2 from the tenth pet of a chain on; a tickle is 3, whatever the chain. Paid only for a pet or a tickle that `touch` pays today: a living creature at home (no `job`), awake (a night sleep and a nap both pay nothing), in Adult Mode. Where a pet can happen is the Stage's rule (the home and a field; the habitat and the arena refuse in the Stage, `touch` does not look at the place).
- **The taper of happiness** (replaces the 15 an hour cap for Adult Mode): per window of `windowMinutes`, a gain is the full amount while the window's gained points are under 15, half while under 30, a quarter after (`[tune]`): pets pay 2 eight times, then 1 fourteen times, then 0.5; tickles 3 five times, 1.5 ten times, then 0.75. The window restarts when `now - windowStart >= 60 minutes` **or when `now < windowStart`** (the clock went back). A pet is never worth 0, so the heart always floats; at 100 happiness a pet still uses the window and still pays XP, as it does today.
- **The Kid path is the old code, line for line** (the 15 an hour cap, the old window test, no `bond` key written); the branch is `isAdultMode(save)`, read on every call.
- **State:** `Creature.bond?: { xp: number; chain: number; at: number }`, optional; missing means level 1 with nothing saved. `xp` is a whole number from 0 to 15,000, **`chain` is stored saturating at 10** (a longer hold still pays 2 and stores 10), `at` is the game time of the last pet. About 40 characters.
- **The chain:** `gap = now - at`; the chain continues (chain + 1, capped at 10) when `0 <= gap <= 2,000` ms and otherwise restarts at 1, so a negative gap (the clock went back) restarts it and a gap of 0 (two touches in the same millisecond) continues it. Only a pet sets `at` and the chain; a poke and a tickle leave both alone.
- **Level-up:** `touch` returns `levelUp` only when a level was just reached (the key does not exist otherwise, so every existing `toEqual` on the result keeps passing); the Stage shows a sparkle burst and the HUD line, and one line goes to the dev log (at most nine per creature).

### Edge cases (a test for each; "(b)" is a browser check)

1. Kid Mode: `touch` gives the old numbers exactly (the 15 an hour total, no `bond` key written, a result of the old shape) and the screen is the same as `main` (b: the Kid comparison, 0 differences). The existing tests listed in section 2 pass unchanged.
2. Adult Mode, the taper: pets pay 2 eight times, 1 fourteen times, then 0.5; tickles 3 five times, 1.5 ten times, then 0.75; a new window after 60 minutes; the heart shows on every pet (b).
3. A pet or a tickle on a creature that is asleep (night or nap), dead, an egg or away on a job gives no XP and does not touch the chain.
4. XP: a pet adds 1 up to chain 9 and 2 from chain 10; a tickle adds 3; the total stops at 15,000; XP never goes down; the level is a function of XP alone and level 10 stays level 10.
5. The chain: two pets 2,000 ms apart chain, 2,001 ms apart do not; a gap of 0 chains; a clock that goes backwards (the device clock) restarts the chain at 1 and never makes a negative gap; a tickle or a poke between two pets changes nothing; a pet and a tickle at the same instant each do their own work.
6. The stored chain: a hold of 150 pets stores `chain` 10 and, after `migrateSave`, keeps its XP, its level and its chain (a longer hold never saves what the loader refuses).
7. Level-ups: crossing a threshold returns that level once, and the next pet returns no `levelUp`; no step can skip a level (the largest step is 3, the smallest gap between two levels is 300, L1 to L2).
8. A new egg, a death and a retire: the next creature has no `bond` key and is level 1.
9. Saves: a missing `bond` loads as level 1; a damaged one (a string, negative or fractional `xp`, `NaN`, `Infinity`, above the cap, a `chain` over 10 or fractional, a non-finite or negative `at`, extra keys, `constructor`, an array) is dropped whole, never throws and never keeps half of it; a Kid profile that has `bond` data keeps it untouched and shows nothing; `version` stays 3.
10. The window and the clock (Adult): set the clock back inside a window and the window restarts, so the taper starts again at full (a mutant removes the `now < windowStart` test and a test must fail).
11. Happiness already 100: a pet gives `gained` and uses the window as today, pays XP, and the heart shows; happiness stays 100.
12. The place: a creature in a field is paid like one at home (unit, `touch` is called directly); in the habitat and the arena a hold starts nothing (b, the Stage refuses it).
13. A mode change in the middle of a hold: Adult Mode switched off between two pets makes the second pet follow the Kid rule and leaves `bond` as it was; a Kid profile that carries `bond` data gets neither the L2 bounce nor the L5 burst (the idle and burst code read `isAdultMode`); a profile switch with a finger down does not carry the chain over.
14. The dev time speed: `at` is game time, so above about 2.5 times speed a held finger's 800 ms reads as more than 2,000 ms and the chain does not form; this is dev mode only and goes in known-issues (a test with `now` values shows the break).
15. Sync, the passive device: Bond changes only through `game.act` (a player action), so a pet marks the device as changed and is sent like any play; a device that only carried its clock forward is not marked changed and adopts the newer server save (`noteSave`, `planAfterPull`): a test in the style of `tests/game-save-hook.test.ts` shows a pet is a player save and a tick is not. An older build (`main`) that loads a save with `bond` keeps the field as it is (`migrateSave` passes a creature through without rebuilding it, so an unknown field survives a save) and earns nothing into it; a newer build then continues from the XP that is there. Nothing is lost, which is unlike the Playroom's item (known-issues G20).
16. The display (b): Adult Mode only; a name, a level, the progress to the next level and the next unlock that exists, readable at 320, 360 and 390 at pixel ratios 1, 2 and 3 with no clipped text and no sideways scroll (measured with a Range, not `scrollWidth`); the label for a screen reader reads "Bond level 3, 120 of 700 to level 4"; Kid Mode shows nothing new.
17. Poke in Adult Mode says "Poke: gets attention" in the stat or care help text; it still pays nothing and still startles (b); a poke on a napping creature does nothing, as today.
18. The unlocks: L2 makes the creature bounce for joy and show a heart now and then, on its own, when it is in a good mood (a test of the rolls with a pinned random source, and none below L2, in Kid Mode or in low spirits); L5 makes a pet's burst bigger; neither is shown to Kid Mode.
19. Growth is not skewed by accident: a 5 minute petting session in Adult Mode is measured against the old rule for the happiness cells it adds (a test over the real `countCareCells` of `game/growth.ts` and `fieldCells`); the number goes in decisions.md. If it changes which adult a creature becomes by a margin the owner has not seen, it is a question for the owner, not a quiet change.
20. The result's shape: every `touch` result that is not a level-up has exactly the old keys (the `toEqual` in `tests/jobs.test.ts` line 263 and `tests/actions.test.ts` line 224 are the net).

## 10. A3.2 in detail: Bug Catch and the training session (plan v4, written 2026-11-25 before any code; v1, v2 and v3 were each reviewed by a cheap model, 33, 19 and 14 findings; sections 11, 12 and 13 say what changed)

### What is true today (grepped on 2026-11-25; file and line as read)

1. **Training exists only as fight winnings, and never for Speed.** `addTrained(c, stat, points)` (`game/combat/stats.ts:24`) adds to `Creature.trained` (`state.ts:87`, an optional `Partial<Record<CombatStat, number>>`), never past `combatTuning.trainedCap[stage]` (`data/combat.ts:14`: baby 0, kid 12, young 20, adult 40), and returns the points it really added. A win trains one stat by `statGain` 1 (a boss win 2): attack trains Strength, guard Defense, special Spirit (`combat/fight.ts`, `trains`). Nothing trains Speed or Vitality, so Bug Catch's Speed is a new source, not a second one. Half the points carry over at an evolution (`sim.ts:125`).
2. **Discipline** is `Creature.stats.discipline`, 30 at hatching (`data/tuning.ts:22`), 0 to 100; nothing raises it. Combat changes its behaviour from it (`sulkChance`, `combat/battle.ts:54`: below 50, 0.01 a point, at most 0.3); the Adult details card (`game/hud.ts:203` to 208) and the dev panel (`ui/DevPanel.tsx:27`) only read it.
3. **Refusals** are a function from the creature to a code or null (`fightRefusal`, `combat/fight.ts:22`: `busy` = away on a job, `asleep`, `sick`, `too-young` = below `combatTuning.minStage` (kid), `tired`); the sentences are in `game/reasons.ts` (`fightReason`, line 84; `awayReason` and `asleepReason` are private to that file, so `trainReason` goes in it too); Adult buttons ask `game.whyNot(refusal, reason)` (`ui/game.ts:264`, `ArenaBar.tsx:88`), which says the sentence over the room and is always null in Kid Mode.
4. **The Arena bar** before a fight is a note row and one wide `Fight` button (`ui/ArenaBar.tsx:148` to 160, `.btn.wide.fight`, 76 px tall); its file header says "Icons only". Adult words under icons come from `Word` (`ui/Icon.tsx`), used by the nav and the care row, not by the Arena. There is no bug icon (`IconName`, `art/sprites.ts:25`).
5. **A fight's state is `Game.arena`**, not in the save. `Game.changed()` (`ui/game.ts:280`) marks the device as changed by the player (unless `byPlayer` is false), schedules a save and a cloud upload, and clears `arena` when the creature is gone or has a different form; `replaceSave` (`:306`) clears it; `setScreen` refuses while a fight is on; `act(fn)` (`:299`) runs `fn` on the save at `now` and then calls `changed()`; `say(text)` and the view-only starts (`startDecor`) wake the screens without `changed()`.
6. **`finishFight`** logs one line to the dev log and calls `noteRequest` (`combat/fight.ts`). A training session will log one line at its end and will add no request kind (A3.2 does not touch the board).
7. **A local calendar day** was `dayNumber(ms)`, private to `game/forecast.ts` (line 289); `game/time.ts`, the shared time helper, had no day function. The first build step of this slice moved it to `time.ts` as the exported `localDay` (`forecast.ts` imports it; done). Tests set a daylight-saving zone with `process.env.TZ = "America/New_York"` (`tests/forecast.test.ts:190`, `tests/hud.test.ts:353`).

6. **The engine is a pure function of a seed and time** (`game/training.ts`, no `Date`, no `Math.random`): `newBugCatch(seed, stage)`, `step(state, dtMs)`, `catchBug(state, id)`, `missTap(state)`, `bandOf(score, stage)`, `payTraining(save, c, run, nowMs)`. **Fixed internal step of 10 ms** (the state carries the leftover), so the same seed gives the same run at 60 and 120 Hz; **each call's `dtMs` is capped at 100 ms** inside `step` (a known effect: on a device under 10 frames a second the engine runs slower than real time, a 75-second session taking 150 seconds at 5 frames a second; the cap is there so a stalled page cannot jump the bugs; it goes in known-issues). 7. **The field and the starting values** (all `[tune]`, one row per stage in `data/training.ts`). The field is a square of 1000 by 1000 engine units drawn as large as the overlay leaves it: the overlay uses `.panel-body` (`app.css:596`, max-width 480 and 16 px side padding, with `box-sizing: border-box` on every element, `app.css:28`), so the field is at most 448 px wide, and 320 - 32 = 288 px in the narrowest window in use. A short, wide window (a phone on its side, 740 by 360) puts the field on the left, as large a square as the height allows, and the timer, score, Pause and Quit in a column on the right, the way the Shop's landscape rule does (`app.css`, `orientation: landscape`); the whole field is on screen without scrolling at every size in use (edge 20). The sizes will be measured on the mock; a catch is made by the screen, not by distance, so the engine has no hit radius (the stand-in player uses a model radius, below). Bugs move in legs of 600 to 1200 ms in a straight line at the row's speed, then take a new heading from the seed; a bug that would leave the field bounces off the edge, so none is ever outside; a bug is gone when its lifetime ends (it is not a miss and not a catch). The spawn timer starts at 400 ms and counts down every step; when it reaches 0 a bug spawns if fewer than the most are alive, at a random spot at least 60 units from the edge, and the timer is set to the row's gap (so a kid run spawns at 400, 1,800 and 3,200 ms); if the field is full the timer waits at 0 and the bug spawns on the first step a place is free, and the timer is then set to the gap. A golden bug lives as long as a plain one. A golden bug is a different shape (bigger, a star on its back), 1.5 times faster, worth 30 against 10.

   | | kid | young | adult |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | speed (units a second) | 160 | 220 | 280 |
   | gap between spawns (ms) | 1400 | 1100 | 700 |
   | most bugs at once | 3 | 4 | 5 |
   | lifetime (ms) | 5000 | 4200 | 3600 |
   | golden chance of a spawn | 12% | 14% | 15% |

   A baby has no row (refused: too young). The row is chosen from the stage at the start; the cap is read from the stage at the pay.
8. **Score and bands.** A catch scores `base × (streak >= 5 ? 2 : 1)` where `base` is 10 (30 for golden) and `streak` is the number of catches since the last miss **including this one**, so the 5th catch of a streak is the first doubled one; a miss (a tap on the field with no bug) sets the streak to 0 and costs no points; an expired bug changes neither. **Bands:** Bronze below the Silver line, Silver from it up to below the Gold line, Gold from the Gold line (`>=`), one pair of lines per stage. The lines are written from the stand-in player's runs, not guessed: `tools/verify/train-bands.ts` (the same pattern as `growth-bond.ts`) prints the score percentiles of 2000 seeded runs per stage, Gold is the line that 20 to 30 percent of runs reach (spec 4's target for an average adult's first day) and Silver is the median; the literals go into `data/training.ts` and into decisions.md, and a test pins them. The stand-in is a model (`tests/trainBot.ts`): it sees a bug 450 to 850 ms after it appears, taps where the bug was 200 ms before the tap, off by a miss drawn evenly from a disc of 115 units, at most once every 480 ms (one thumb), tries a missed bug again after that gap, and catches it when that spot is within 76 units of the bug (half of the 153-unit button of decision 4, which is 44 px at the narrowest field, 288 px; the button scales with the field, so the model holds from a 288 px field up, and on a narrower one the 44 px floor makes the target bigger than the model); say if it feels too hard or easy (question 3). A session with no catch is not a session: it pays nothing and counts for nothing.
9. **Rewards** (spec 4: "full stat reward for the first 3 sessions per stat per day, half reward for the next 3, then bond XP only"). `n` is the number of Speed sessions already counted today. n < 3: full; 3 <= n < 6: half; n >= 6: Bond XP only. Full points by band: Bronze 1, Silver 1, Gold 2 `[tune]`; half is the full amount rounded down, so Bronze and Silver pay 0 and Gold pays 1. The points go through `addTrained`, so the stage cap holds (at the cap it adds 0 and the card says so). **Bond XP** for every counted session: Bronze 10, Silver 20, Gold 30 `[tune]`, through a new `addBondXp(c, amount)` in `bond.ts` that adds, caps at `XP_MAX`, leaves the chain and the last-pet time alone and returns the level reached or null (`earnBond` is changed to share the add-and-level code, and its behaviour does not change). The spec's "4 to 5 days of 3 full-reward sessions" is for "one stat, untrained to combat-ready" (spec 4, line 262), which is below the kid cap or about it; the plan does not claim it equals the cap.
10. **The day count is a creature field:** `training?: { day: number; sessions: Partial<Record<CombatStat, number>> }`, optional, coerced by a new `coerceTraining` in `migrateSave` (a damaged one is dropped, never half kept; `day` an integer >= 0, each count an integer 0 to 6; only `vit str def spi spd` keys). Only `spd` is written in A3.2; the shape keeps the other two minigames (A4) in the same field. `day` is `localDay(now)`, an integer from a new exported `localDay` in `game/time.ts` (`forecast.ts`'s `dayNumber` moves there and `forecast.ts` imports it; its behaviour does not change). The count stored is `min(n + 1, 6)`. **The rule for a stored day `d` against `today`:** `d < today` starts again (fresh counts, day = today); `d === today` adds to the counts; `d === today + 1` (a clock set back a day, or a trip west) keeps the counts **and keeps `d`**, and a session there adds to them, so a set-back of one day never refills the full-reward sessions on that device; `d > today + 1` is treated as damaged and starts again with day = today, so one device with a wrong clock cannot lock Speed rewards on every device for years. A deliberate set-back of two days or more does refill the full-reward sessions: they pace the game, they are not a protected score (known-issues). The rule is per device clock: two devices that disagree on the day can each start the count again (a device a day ahead reads the stored day as the past and rewrites it; the other device then reads it as tomorrow and keeps it), so full rewards can pass the cap by a day's worth then, the same known limit. A new egg has no `training` field. The start card reads the count with the same rule.

It does nothing when there is no session, when the run is not over, or when the session already has a result (that last test is the guard against a second pay: a double call, a double tap, a frame that sees `over` twice). Otherwise it ticks the game first, like `whyNot` (so a death or the end of a job in the gap is seen; a death clears the session and it ends there), and then calls `game.act` once, the only `act` in a session: `payTraining` pays, logs one dev-log line and returns the result. `finishTraining` stores the result on `Game.training` (or closes the overlay when `payTraining` returned null) and then wakes the screens by hand, because `act`'s own `changed()` ran before the store. Only start, Go, finish and close wake the app through `Game`'s listeners (start mounts the overlay, Go starts the run and the overlay's clock, finish fills the result and close unmounts it); pause, resume, every catch, every miss and every frame stay inside the overlay component (refs, and its own state when the list of bugs changes) and never reach `Game`'s listeners or `changed()`, so a session adds no write of its own: with the game's clock held still, a session in progress leaves the save and the dev log as they were, and every `changed` call during it is the clock's own (`changed(false)`, from `tick`). Time keeps passing as it does in a fight (`tick` runs once a second and is not suspended), so in real play a minute boundary inside a session changes the save (the stats decay) just as it would without one. "Done" and Quit on the result card both just close it. Quit while playing pays nothing. `payTraining` pays if there is still a living creature that is not away (`job`), whatever its sleep or sickness by then, and returns null otherwise; `finishTraining` then closes the overlay with no card. It stores the result on `Game.training` **after** `act` has run `changed()`, so the clear of decision 12 cannot take it. The seed is `Math.floor(Math.random() * 2 ** 32)` at the start and never touches `save.rngState`.

### Decisions for A3.3 (mine, each changeable)

1. **State: view state on `Game`, never saved.** `Game.initiative: Initiative | null` and the countdown are not in the save, not in the dev log, and never go through `changed()`. Creating, fading, answering a play toss and finishing wake the screens through `Game`'s listeners only (like Bug Catch's start and close); the answer itself (a payment) is one `act`. The draws use `Game.rand: () => number`, a plain field that starts as `Math.random` and that the tests set (so a draw can be forced); the save's generator (`rngState`) is never touched.
2. **Time: real foreground time.** `Game.tick()` adds `min(real - lastReal, 2000)` ms to an `initiativeClock` before it moves `lastReal`, so dev time speed does not change the cadence, and a hidden page (whose interval is throttled to about once a minute) adds at most 2 seconds a tick. All the timing below counts that clock. A new profile or save (`replaceSave`) resets it.
3. **Cadence, start to start.** A countdown of `60,000 + floor(rand() × 30,001)` ms (60,000 to 90,000 both ends) counts the clock's milliseconds on which the game is neither blocked nor unseen (decision 4). At zero an initiative is made and a new countdown is drawn at once, so the spec's "one every 60 to 90 seconds" is the gap between starts; an ignoring tester sees 3 to 5 in 300 seconds. An initiative ends when answered or 30,000 ms after it was made (a play that has begun has its own length, decision 8, and ends at most 45,000 ms after it was made), both under the shortest countdown, so two never overlap. While blocked or unseen the countdown holds, and an active initiative that is blocked is dropped with no cost (a drop does not change the countdown). The first countdown is drawn at the first tick at which the game is not blocked. `tick()` is the only caller of `stepInitiative(dtMs)`; the tests use both `tick()` (fake timers) and `stepInitiative` directly.
4. **Blocked** (an initiative cannot be made and an active one is dropped) is any of: not Adult Mode; no living creature; away; asleep; `call !== null`; `screen !== "room"`; an `arena`, `training`, `decor` or `view.moment`; a waiting haul card; `menuOpen`; a meal in progress (`view.snack !== null`). `menuOpen` is a plain field on `Game` that `App` writes from `panel !== null` in an effect (the only way `Game` sees the panel). A held finger, the feed tray and the Adult header's own cards are not blockers (the tray is how a craving is answered; the cards sit above the picture: read from the layout, shown by a contact sheet state). A creature in a field is not blocked (the room screen's line world). **Unseen** (the countdown holds, but an active initiative is not dropped) is a creature that has walked out of the camera's view: `Stage` sets `game.view.inView` every frame from `inView(cam, box, vp)` (true in the line world and until the Stage first sets it). The drop is made at once by `setScreen`, `startDecor`, `startTraining` and `replaceSave` (they call `dropInitiative()`), and by the next tick for the rest (at most a second later); `answerInitiative()` asks the same test, so a bubble tapped in that gap pays nothing and clears.
5. **A craving pays Bond XP, not a doubled serving.** The spec's reward ("that feeding counts double toward its food cell") would let a plain meal add a whole cell without a booster, and the Growth Forecast's bound (`forecast.ts:271,493`) assumes a plain meal adds one serving; the bound would then call a state "locked" that a craving could still change. Changing that bound changes the forecast's pinned results (A1.2's brute-force tests). So a craving pays Bond XP 8, and the double count is an owner question (below) with the exact change it needs. This is a deviation from the spec, recorded in decisions.md.
6. **Craving.** The food is drawn from `menuOf(c)` without the treat (it can make the creature sick; a craving must not ask for it) among foods `feedRefusal(c, food) === null` accepts at that moment (so not while it is full); a craving is a food the menu offers, so the spec's "sometimes a shop or recipe food" is not built. Any meal of that food answers it, plain or a dish whose `eats` is that food. A new `Game.feed(food, dish?)` does what `ActionBar.onFood` did (`act(feed)`) and, in the same `act`, pays the craving when the result is `"ate"` and the food matches; it returns `{ result, levelUp }` (`levelUp` is the Bond level the craving's XP reached, or null) and `ActionBar` calls it and throws the same sparkles and heart a pet's level-up does. A different food, or a refused feed, changes nothing and the craving stays. The bubble shows the food's icon; it is not a tap target (the hit test below skips it): the player answers it by feeding.
7. **Gift.** The gift is drawn when the initiative is made: 1 coin in 3 is 2 coins, otherwise 1 of the five materials (an even draw), and the bubble shows that item's icon (`coin`, or the material's). A tap on the bubble pays it once through one `act` and says it in a note ("Found 1 Herb." or "Found 2 coins."); the spec's "nudges a leaf, shell or shiny it found in the field" (a framing and an animation) is not built. At the rate of one gift per three or four minutes of open play it is about half of a first job's pay, so no daily cap is built (known-issues).
8. **Play invite.** The bubble is a new 8x8 `ball` icon. A tap on it is toss 1: the creature hops and sparkles, Bond XP +2 (through one `act` and `addBondXp`, a level reached is shown by the existing burst), and a 15,000 ms play starts (the low end of the spec's 15 to 20 seconds); each further tap on the ball (it stays over the creature while the play lasts) is another toss, +2 XP, up to 6 tosses (12 XP in all). The play ends at the sixth toss or 15,000 ms after the first; the countdown is already running (decision 3). A play with no tap after the first is still paid for the tosses made. The spec's "toss-or-chase" is the toss only. No request type exists for play, so the spec's "request progress" is not built (known-issues).
9. **Weights, by form and Bond.** A kind's weight is its base (play 3, craving 2, gift 2) plus a step from the form id (a stable hash of the id gives each kind 0, 1 or 2, the same for one form always) plus `0.25 × (bondLevel - 1)` for play. The draw walks the kinds in the fixed order play, craving, gift: `r = rand() × total`, subtract each weight, and take the first kind whose running total is above `r` (strict, so a kind with weight 0 is never taken); a kind that is not possible now (a craving with nothing to ask for) is given weight 0 for the draw only. `preferenceOf(c)` is static (the form and the Bond level, never what is possible now) and names the two heaviest kinds in weight order, ties in the order play, craving, gift: the Bond card gets one line, "Prefers: play, cravings." (`hud.ts`, `bondLines`).
10. **Where it is drawn and tapped.** `View` gets `invite: IconName | null` (looks only); `Game` sets it whenever the initiative changes; `drawScene` draws it as a bubble in the call's slot, steady with a one pixel bob (the call blinks, so the two read differently; the call cannot be active at the same time, decision 4). `art/scene.ts` exports `bubbleBox(x, y, size, spotsW)`, the same clamp as the draw, which `Stage` uses for the hit test: a pointer down on a play or gift bubble (box plus the same pad as the creature) makes a `Hold` with `bubble: true` and no pet timer, and the up of a tap (in the home only if the gesture tracker says tap, not pan) calls `game.answerInitiative()` and **returns before `tap()`**, so a ball tap neither pokes nor counts toward a tickle (in the line world the same). A pan or a second finger ends the hold as it does for the creature. The bubble is tested before the creature, so a poke or a pet on the creature itself is unchanged. **The hit test and the draw share one position:** `scene.ts` exports `bubbleBox(view, size, spotsW)`, which adds `poseOffsets(view)` to `view.x` and `view.y - size` exactly as the draw does and applies the call's clamp; both the draw and `Stage` use it (the creature's own hit test keeps its unoffset box and pad, as today). The canvas gets the attributes `data-initiative` (the kind) and `data-bubble` (the box in CSS pixels, written every frame) **only while an initiative is showing, and removed otherwise** (never present in Kid Mode, so the Kid compare is unchanged), for the browser checks, and an addition to its accessible name ("Muffin is holding out a ball: tap it to play").
11. **Answering is exactly once.** `answerInitiative()` does nothing unless Adult Mode is on, an initiative of kind play or gift exists and the game is not blocked (a blocked game clears it and pays nothing); a gift is taken and cleared before it pays, so a second call (a double tap in one frame) finds nothing. A play toss checks the toss count before it pays.
12. **Clearing.** `changed()` drops the initiative only when the creature is gone (not alive), another one hatched (`bornAt` differs from the one it was made for) or Adult Mode is off, the way it drops a training session; it does **not** drop it on every call, because a toss, a pet, a feed or a purchase is an `act` and goes through `changed()`. `replaceSave` (an account's save, a profile switch), `setScreen`, `startDecor` and `startTraining` call `dropInitiative()`; everything else that blocks is seen by the next tick's test (decision 4). A drop clears the active initiative only; the countdown is not touched, and `replaceSave` also redraws the countdown and zeroes the clock.
13. **Kid Mode gets nothing:** no initiative, no `data-initiative` or `data-bubble` attribute, no line on a card. The Kid compare must show 0 differences.
14. **Accessibility is as petting is.** The canvas bubble is a pointer target like the creature; the accessible name says what is offered; a keyboard player cannot answer a play or gift initiative (known-issues, as for petting). A craving is answered with the ordinary feed buttons.
15. **Dev panel:** nothing new (YAGNI); the tests drive the engine through `tick()` and `stepInitiative(dtMs)` and force a draw through `game.rand`.
16. **Not built:** the Trick moment, Show me and Nap/quiet time kinds (the spec lists six, the plan three); the chase in the play invite (a toss only) and its 15 to 20 seconds (15); a shop or recipe food as a craving; the nudge framing of a gift; a request tick from play; the Book clue on a gift (Spec 8); a daily cap; a push notification (the spec forbids it in this phase).
