# A4 "Master it": plan (written 2026-11-26 on branch `adult-a4`, before any A4 code)

CLAUDE.md section 9 defines A4 as "the other two minigames, timed combat, Book recipes, the perfect run, legacy". The spec parts are Spec 4 (the three games), Spec 7 (combat) and Spec 8 (the endgame), in docs/adult-expansion-spec.md. This file holds the slicing and, as `adult-a3-plan.md` did, one detailed section per slice that is written just before the slice is built. Today only A4.1 has one (section 2).

## 1. Slices (each its own branch and pull request, each ends with the owner merging; I never merge)

The order is by dependency. Each slice is playable and testable alone.

| Slice | Ships | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **A4.1 Rhythm Grove** (Spec 4, game 2) | A game picker on the training start card, Rhythm Grove (Spirit): a song per field, Perfect and Good windows in milliseconds, bands, the Spirit reward through the existing session shell | Its timing windows and feedback words are the ones timed combat reuses (spec 4: "reuse the same windows and feedback words"), so it comes first |
| **A4.2 Stack & Balance** (Spec 4, game 3) | The third game (Strength) on the same picker and shell | Copies A4.1's wiring, so it is cheap once A4.1 is proven |
| **A4.3 Timed combat** (Spec 7) | Timed attack, timed guard, the special as a decision, the sulk rule, the Adult Mode numbers | Needs the windows from A4.1 and trained stats from A4.1 and A4.2 (the balance targets are about trained creatures) |
| **A4.4 Book recipes and the perfect run** (Spec 8) | Book recipes (what a form needs, as a page), the perfect-run record and badge | Reads care counters that exist today; independent of A4.1 to A4.3 |
| **A4.5 Legacy** (Spec 8) | Retire then inherit at hatching: a tint, or +3 to one stat, or a favourite toy | Needs retire (A1.4, merged) and the Book records; last because it changes the hatch |

A5 (secret evolutions) follows, planned the same way. Sections for A4.2 to A4.5 are written when each is next.

## 2. A4.1 in detail: Rhythm Grove (plan v1, written 2026-11-26)

Spec 4's second game: notes fall in time and the player taps on the beat while the creature dances; the Perfect, Good and Miss windows are shown in milliseconds in Adult Mode (`[tune: Perfect +-80 ms]`); the song is a short loop, different per field; it trains Spirit; it doubles as the timing tutor for combat. It must also meet the shell's rules: 60 to 90 seconds, one thumb, playable with sound off, pause on app-switch, no energy cost, rewards taper (3 full, 3 half, then Bond XP only) per stat, Bronze, Silver and Gold bands with Gold paying more Discipline, difficulty from the creature's stage.

### Decisions for A4.1 (mine, each changeable)

1. **The picker is part of the start card, not a new screen.** The Train button stays as it is in the Arena row (its label becomes "Train: play a training game"). The start card gets a row of one button per game (`role="group"`, `aria-pressed`): Bug Catch and Rhythm Grove. The game on show decides the blurb, the stat line and what Go begins. `Training` gets `game: "bug" | "rhythm"`; `Game.trainPick` (not saved, starts as `"bug"`) remembers the last pick between sessions in the same visit; `Game.pickTrainGame(id)` works only in phase `ready` and ignores an unknown id. A burst is always Bug Catch and never reads or changes the pick.
2. **A table of games in the data**, `trainGames` in `src/data/training.ts`: `{ bug: { name: "Bug Catch", stat: "spd", statName: "Speed" }, rhythm: { name: "Rhythm Grove", stat: "spi", statName: "Spirit" } }`. A4.2 adds `stack`. The words "Speed" in `startLine` and `resultLines` come from it.
3. **One pay path.** The middle of `payTraining` (the day count, the tier, `addTrained`, Discipline, Bond XP) moves into one function that takes the game's stat; `payTraining` keeps its signature and its log text, and `payRhythm(save, c, run, nowMs)` calls the same function. `TrainResult` gains `game` and `stat` and, for Rhythm Grove only, `rhythm?: { perfects, goods, missed, best }`. `catches` keeps its name and means "caught, or notes hit". `startLine(stat, count)` takes the stat.
4. **The engine is pure and has no random draw.** A song is a function of the field and the stage, so `newRhythm(field, stage, lengthMs)` takes no seed and a session leaves the save as it was (no `Math.random`). `src/game/rhythm.ts` has `newRhythm`, `stepRhythm(run, dtMs)` (the same contract as `step`: at most `frameCapMs` a call, whole `stepMs` steps, nothing paused or over, true when the set of notes in flight changed or the run ended), `tapRhythm(run)`, `rhythmBandOf`, `maxScore`. `pause` and `resume` are retyped to the shape both states share (`{ paused: boolean; over: boolean }`), not copied.
5. **The song is the creature's field** (`fieldOfForm(c.formId) ?? "land"`), not the field it happens to stand in: a Dustpuff, which belongs to no field, plays Land's. A song is a loop of 16 eighth notes (two bars), a string of 16 characters: `.` a rest, `1` a note at every stage, `2` a note from young up, `3` a note for adults only. Kid plays the 1s, young the 1s and 2s, adult all. Every pattern starts with a `1`. Tempos will be Land 96, Water 84 and Sky 108 beats a minute (an eighth is `30000 / bpm` ms, each note time is rounded to a whole millisecond). The first note is at 2000 ms (the lead-in), notes go on while their time is at most `lengthMs - 1000`, and the run lasts `trainTuning.sessionMs` (75,000 ms). A worked example, Land: `1.1.1.231.1.2.33` (kid notes at eighths 0, 2, 4, 8, 10; young add 6 and 12; adult add 7, 14 and 15). The other two patterns are written at the build under the constraints in the edge cases; each song gets a name that is not shown (YAGNI), only the field decides it.
6. **One lane, tap anywhere.** The whole track is the target (one thumb, no aim, the same on 320 px and 844 px). A note appears at the top `approachMs` (1600) before its time and reaches the line at its time. Notes in flight are at most the 1600 ms window's worth (a handful), so the page draws them from a list that changes only when a note appears or is settled; positions are written through refs every frame like the bugs.
7. **Judging.** A tap is judged at `run.elapsedMs`, the clock the screen shows (whole steps, so within 10 ms of the last frame), against the unsettled note in flight whose time is the closest, a tie going to the earlier one, if it is within `goodMs` of it: at most `perfectMs` away is Perfect, else Good. No note in the window: a stray, which resets the streak, costs no points and takes nothing from any note. A note not tapped is a Miss, settled at the first step where `elapsedMs - t > goodMs`, and resets the streak. `timingWindows = { perfectMs: 80, goodMs: 160 }` `[tune]` lives in `src/data/rhythm.ts` so combat can import it. The latency of the phone's screen is not compensated (known-issues).
8. **Score and bands.** Perfect 100, Good 60, nothing else scores; `maxScore` is 100 times the notes of the song at that stage, so the lines are percentages of it, whole numbers: `silverPct` and `goldPct` per stage, Gold when `score * 100 >= max * goldPct`. They will be written from the percentiles of the stand-in player (`tests/rhythmBot.ts`, printed by `tools/verify/rhythm-bands.ts`), aiming at Gold in about a quarter of the stand-in's runs at each stage (spec 4: 20 to 30 percent for an average adult on day 1); its timing error will be a normal draw whose spread grows with the density of the stage's notes, which is an assumption to be written next to it. Mashing every beat alone reaches Good at best, so at most 60 percent of the maximum: the Silver line will be above that.
9. **Rewards are the shell's, unchanged**: the same `points`, `bondXp` and `discipline` tables, the count per stat (so three Speed sessions do not use up Spirit's three), a run with no note hit pays nothing and counts for nothing (like a Bug Catch with no catch). A game's text says "Spirit". The result card adds one line, "Perfect 52, Good 19, missed 10, best streak 23".
10. **The creature dances.** The creature's open face (`faceSprite(form, "open")`) sits beside the line and hops on every Perfect or Good; nothing happens for a stray or a Miss (no punishment, no sad face). The hop is a CSS animation switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`; the word still shows.
11. **Words, not colours.** Each tap writes one short line over the line, in words: "Perfect", "Good", "Miss" for a missed note, "No note" for a stray, and in Adult Mode (all of training is Adult Mode) the offset: "Perfect, 12 ms early", "Good, 95 ms late". The start card says "Perfect is within 80 ms, Good within 160 ms" from the tuning, not typed. The line is an element with `data-judge` and `data-delta` for the checks; it is not a live region (four updates a second would flood a screen reader), and the result card is.
12. **A new icon, `note`** (an 8 x 8 eighth note), is the picture of a note on the track and the Rhythm Grove button's icon.
13. **Kid Mode gets nothing.** Training is Adult Mode only already; the Kid compare must show 0 differences.
14. **Not built (each recorded in decisions.md):** sound (no hit tones: the game is silent, per spec "sound off"), a latency calibration screen, lanes, hold notes, a combo multiplier, a "free play" with no pay, request-board cards for Rhythm Grove, play invites that offer it (the spec's "creature play invites can offer it" is not built for Bug Catch either: a play invite is a ball toss (`answerInitiative`, `src/ui/game.ts:410`) and opens no session today), and a song picker (the field decides).

### Edge cases (a test for each; "(b)" is a browser check)

Song data (`tests/rhythm.test.ts`):
1. Each pattern is 16 characters of `.`, `1`, `2`, `3` and starts with `1`. The three fields differ in tempo and in pattern.
2. The first note of every field at every stage is at 2000 ms; the last is at most 74,000 ms; times rise strictly; the gap between kept notes is at least 550 ms for kid and young and at least 250 ms for adult; every note is a whole millisecond.
3. The number of notes per stage is in a range (kid 55 to 85, young 80 to 115, adult 110 to 145), and a higher stage has every note of a lower one (the sets only grow).
4. `maxScore` is 100 times that count (a literal for one field and stage).

Judging:
5. The boundaries: a tap 80 ms before or after a note is Perfect, 81 ms is Good, 160 ms is Good, 161 ms is a stray and the note is still there.
6. A stray resets the streak, adds no score, settles no note, and counts in `strays`.
7. A note nobody taps is settled as a Miss at the first whole step after `t + goodMs` (not before: 160 ms late is still tappable) and resets the streak; a whole run with no tap ends with every note a Miss, the score 0 and `over`.
8. Two notes inside one window (adult notes 278 ms apart): a tap goes to the closest; a tie goes to the earlier; a settled note is never judged twice; the next tap judges the other.
9. The streak counts hits since the last miss or stray and `best` is its largest; the score is exactly `100 * perfects + 60 * goods`.
10. Paused: `stepRhythm` and `tapRhythm` do nothing and the clock does not move; resume goes on where it was; over: no tap counts; a tap before the first note appears is a stray.
11. `stepRhythm` fed 7 ms, 16 ms and 100 ms frames reaches the same elapsed time and the same notes in flight for the same total time (up to the frame cap); a stalled frame of 5 s moves the song 100 ms, not 5 s; a non-finite or negative `dtMs` moves it nothing.
12. `rhythmBandOf` at each line (one point under, on, one over) for each stage, from the integer percentages.

Pay and screen (`tests/rhythm-game.test.ts`):
13. `payRhythm` pays Spirit by the tier from the Spirit count, leaves the Speed count and Speed alone, honours the stage cap ("Spirit is as high as it can go for now"), adds Discipline and Bond XP by band, writes one log line, and pays nothing for a run with no hit. `payTraining`'s numbers and log text are unchanged (the existing tests stay as they are).
14. The refusals: a creature that left, died or was replaced between the start and the finish pays nobody (`finishTraining` on a session whose creature changed); a second `finishTraining` pays nothing more.
15. `pickTrainGame`: only in phase `ready`; an unknown id changes nothing; the pick survives `closeTraining` within the visit; `startBurst` starts Bug Catch whatever the pick and leaves the pick alone.
16. `startLine` and `resultLines` for each game and tier (the words "Speed" and "Spirit" from the table, the result line for a no-hit run, the extra rhythm line).
17. A song for a form with no field is Land's; for each real field its own.
18. (b) the picker: Bug Catch is pressed first; Rhythm Grove shows its blurb, "Spirit: full reward, 1 of 3 today" and the window numbers; Go begins the game on show.
19. (b) the beat: with a frozen clock, a tap at the first note's time (2000 ms into the run) reads Perfect; a tap 100 ms late reads Good, late; a tap with no note near reads "No note" and resets the streak; the expected times are the checker's own literals from the tempo and the pattern, not read from the page.
20. (b) a scripted run: tapping a chosen set of notes at their exact times gives the exact score, the result card's words and a Spirit gain, all literals; the day count for Spirit goes up by one and Speed's does not.
21. (b) pause on a hidden page stops the notes and the clock; "Tap to go on" resumes; reload in the middle pays nothing.
22. (b) touch: a real finger tap on the track counts, a drag does not scroll the page behind, in a mobile window.
23. (b) layout at 320, 360 and 390 at ratio 1 and 2 and on the side (740 x 360, 844 x 390): no overflow past `innerWidth`, the track and the buttons inside the panel, the note and the line and the creature inside the track, buttons at least 44 px, every string measured with a Range.
24. (b) names: the dialog is labelled with the game on show, the picker buttons and the track have names, the result card is announced.
25. (b) Kid Mode: no Train button, no `data-judge` anywhere.
26. A Kid Mode game and a Kid save are untouched: `tools/verify/kid-compare.mjs` reports 0 differences against `main`.
