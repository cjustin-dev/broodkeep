The spec parts are Spec 4 (the three games), Spec 7 (combat) and Spec 8 (the endgame), in docs/adult-expansion-spec.md. ## 2. A4.1 in detail: Rhythm Grove (plan v3, written 2026-11-26 before any code; v1 (commit 170f666) went to a cheap-model reviewer, 16 findings, section 3 says what changed; v2 (7b7c266) went to a second one, 9 findings, section 4 says what changed)

Spec 4's second game: notes fall in time and the player taps on the beat while the creature dances; the Perfect, Good and Miss windows are shown in milliseconds in Adult Mode (`[tune: Perfect +-80 ms]`); the song is a short loop, different per field; it trains Spirit; it doubles as the timing tutor for combat. It must also meet the shell's rules: 60 to 90 seconds, one thumb, playable with sound off, pause on app-switch, no energy cost, rewards taper (3 full, 3 half, then Bond XP only) per stat, Bronze, Silver and Gold bands with Gold paying more Discipline, difficulty from the creature's stage.

### Decisions for A4.1 (mine, each changeable)

1. **The picker is part of the start card, not a new screen.** The Train button stays in the Arena row (its label becomes "Train: play a training game"; spec 4 asks for the home screen, a deviation taken in A3.2 and kept). The start card gets a row of one button per game (`role="group"`, `aria-pressed`): Bug Catch and Rhythm Grove. The game on show decides the blurb, the stat line and what Go begins. `Training` gets `game: TrainGameId` (`"bug" | "rhythm"`) and its `run` becomes `BugCatchState | RhythmState | null`, told apart by `game`; `Game.trainPick` (not saved, starts as `"bug"`) remembers the last pick between sessions in the same visit; `Game.pickTrainGame(id)` works only in phase `ready` and ignores an unknown id. A burst is always Bug Catch and never reads or changes the pick.
2. **A table of games in the data**, `trainGames` in `src/data/training.ts`, keyed by game: `{ bug: { name: "Bug Catch", stat: "spd", statName: "Speed" }, rhythm: { name: "Rhythm Grove", stat: "spi", statName: "Spirit" } }`. A4.2 adds `stack`. The words "Speed" and "Spirit" in `startLine` and `resultLines`, and the dialog's label (`TrainGame.tsx:21`, `:184` and `:198` are hard-coded "Bug Catch" now), come from it by game.
3. **One pay path.** The middle of `payTraining` (the day count, the tier, `addTrained`, Discipline, Bond XP) moves into one function that takes the game's stat; `payTraining` keeps its signature and its log text, and `payRhythm(save, c, run, nowMs)` calls the same function. `TrainResult` gains `game` and `stat` and, for Rhythm Grove only, `rhythm?: { perfects, goods, missed, strays, best }`. `catches` keeps its name and means "caught, or notes hit". `startLine(count)` becomes `startLine(game, count)`; the one existing test that maps it over 0 to 6 (`tests/training.test.ts:510`) is edited to `startLine("bug", n)` and its expected strings stay as they are.
4. **The engine is pure and has no random draw.** A song is a function of the field and the stage, so `newRhythm(field, stage, lengthMs)` takes no seed and a session leaves the save as it was (no `Math.random`). `src/game/rhythm.ts` has `newRhythm`, `stepRhythm(run, dtMs)` (the same contract as `step`: at most `frameCapMs` a call, whole `stepMs` steps, nothing paused or over, true when the set of notes in flight changed or the run ended), `tapRhythm(run)` (returns `{ judge: "perfect" | "good" | "stray", deltaMs } | null`; null when paused or over), `rhythmBandOf`, `maxScore`. A judged tap (Perfect or Good) settles its note and takes it out of `notes`; the screen redraws the list when `tapRhythm` returns Perfect or Good. `RhythmState` is plain data: `field`, `stage`, `lengthMs`, `elapsedMs`, `carryMs`, `times` (every note's time, rising), `next`, `notes: { id, t }[]` (on the track, in time order), `score`, `perfects`, `goods`, `misses`, `strays`, `streak`, `best`, `said` (the last thing to say, with a count, so the screen can tell a new one), `paused`, `over`; a test writes its own `times` and `notes`. `pause` and `resume` are retyped to the shape both states share (`{ paused: boolean; over: boolean }`), not copied. The overlay's `Run` stays Bug Catch's (it takes a `BugCatchState`) and a new `RhythmRun` takes a `RhythmState`; `TrainGame` picks one by `Training.game`.
5. **The song is the creature's field** (`fieldOfForm(c.formId) ?? "land"`), not the field it happens to stand in: a Dustpuff, which belongs to no field, plays Land's. A song is a loop of 16 eighth notes (two bars), a string of 16 characters: `.` a rest, `1` a note at every stage, `2` a note from young up, `3` a note for adults only. Kid plays the 1s, young the 1s and 2s, adult all. Every pattern starts with a `1`. Tempos will be Land 96, Water 84 and Sky 108 beats a minute (an eighth is `30000 / bpm` ms, each note time is rounded to a whole millisecond). The first note is at 2000 ms (the lead-in), notes go on while their time is at most `lengthMs - 1000`, and the run lasts `trainTuning.sessionMs` (75,000 ms). A worked example, Land: `1.1.1.231.1.2.33` (kid notes at eighths 0, 2, 4, 8, 10; young add 6 and 12; adult add 7, 14 and 15; 73, 102 and 144 notes). Sky's loop is shorter (4,444 ms against Land's 5,000), so its patterns need fewer notes a loop to stay in the same ranges: at most 5 kid, 7 young and 8 adult. The other two patterns are written at the build under the constraints in the edge cases; only the field decides the song.
6. **One lane, tap anywhere.** The whole track is the target (one thumb, no aim, the same on 320 px and 844 px). A note enters `notes` and appears at the top `approachMs` (1600) before its time and reaches the line at its time. The page draws the notes from that list, which changes only when a note appears or settles; positions are written through refs every frame like the bugs.
7. **Judging.** A tap is judged at `run.elapsedMs`, the clock the screen shows (whole steps, so within 10 ms of the last frame; the step and the tap never happen "in the same instant": a tap sees the state as of the last step), against the unsettled note in flight whose time is the closest, a tie going to the earlier one, if it is within `goodMs` of it: at most `perfectMs` away is Perfect, else Good. A note not tapped is a Miss, settled at the first step where `elapsedMs - t > goodMs`, and resets the streak. A tap with no note in its window is a stray: it resets the streak, settles nothing and costs `strayCost` points (decision 8). A second tap on a note that is already settled, a double tap, is a stray like any other (a touch screen does not bounce, so no debounce is built). `timingWindows = { perfectMs: 80, goodMs: 160 }` `[tune]` lives in `src/data/rhythm.ts` so combat can import it. The latency of the phone's screen is not compensated (known-issues).
8. **Score, the stray cost and the bands.** Perfect 100, Good 60, a stray `strayCost` 30 `[tune]`, and the score is read as `max(0, 100 * perfects + 60 * goods - 30 * strays)`, so it never goes below 0 and the order of the events does not matter (a stray before any hit does not push it under). `maxScore` is 100 times the notes of the song at that stage, so the lines are percentages of it, whole numbers: `silverPct` and `goldPct` per stage, Gold when `score * 100 >= max * goldPct`. **Why a stray costs points** (v1 said it cost nothing and that mashing "reaches Good at best, so at most 60 percent": the review showed that false, and `a4/mash.mjs` in the scratchpad confirms it by simulation on Land's kid song): with no cost, a tapper who taps at a steady beat of their own, unrelated to the song, scores 60 percent at an interval of 80 ms or less, 68 at 100, 79 at 150, 84 at 200, 86 at 250, 80 at 320 and 66 at 400; that is as good as the stand-in player with a timing spread of 70 ms (median 86). With a stray cost of 30 the same tapper scores at most 32 percent on the kid song and 61 on the adult one (at 320 ms), and the stand-in loses about one point. The best steady tapper over every interval from 40 to 700 ms at every 10 ms and eight phases is 67.5 percent (the adult Land song at its worst interval; `tools/verify/rhythm-bands.ts` prints it), so the Silver line is 75 for every stage, and a test (edge 14) pins that no steady tapper over that sweep reaches it. Gold is written from the percentiles of the stand-in player (`tests/rhythmBot.ts`, printed by `tools/verify/rhythm-bands.ts`), aiming at Gold in about a quarter of the stand-in's runs at each stage (written: 93, 89 and 85 percent for kid, young and adult, which 21, 26 and 22 percent of the stand-in's 3,000 runs a stage reach; every run of it reaches Silver, its tenth percentile being 87, 83 and 79); its timing error will be a normal draw whose spread grows with the density of the stage's notes, with a few stray taps and 3 percent of notes skipped, which is an assumption to be written next to it. Spec 4's "20 to 30 percent for an average adult on day 1" is about real people: only a playtest can check it, and the plan does not claim it.
9. **Rewards are the shell's, unchanged**: the same `points`, `bondXp` and `discipline` tables, the count per stat (so three Speed sessions do not use up Spirit's three). A run with no note hit pays nothing and counts for nothing, the same rule Bug Catch has for a run with no catch (A3.2, decisions.md). This is a deviation from spec 4's "any completed session pays +small discipline", which is read as a session that did something; it is written into decisions.md when the slice is built. The result card adds one line, "Perfect 52, Good 19, missed 10, no note 3, best streak 23".
10. **The creature dances.** The creature's open face (`faceSprite(form, "open")`) sits beside the line and hops on every Perfect or Good; nothing happens for a stray or a Miss (no sad face). The hop is a CSS animation switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`; the word still shows.
11. **Words, not colours.** Each tap writes one short line over the line, in words: "Perfect", "Good", "Miss" for a missed note, "No note, -30" for a stray (the number is the stray cost from the tuning, not typed), and the offset: "Perfect, 12 ms early", "Good, 95 ms late" (the offset is the step clock's, within 10 ms of the tap's own time, because a tap is judged at `elapsedMs`). The words are spec 4's (Perfect, Good, Miss); spec 7 says Normal for a timed attack that is not in a window, and A4.3 decides how that maps. The start card says "Perfect is within 80 ms, Good within 160 ms, a tap on nothing costs 30" from the tuning, not typed. The line is an element with `data-judge` and `data-delta` for the checks; it is not a live region (four updates a second would flood a screen reader), and the result card is.
12. **A new icon, `note`** (an 8 x 8 eighth note), is the picture of a note on the track and the Rhythm Grove button's icon.
13. **Kid Mode gets nothing.** Training is Adult Mode only already; the Kid compare must show 0 differences.
14. **Not built (each recorded in decisions.md):** sound (no hit tones: the game is silent, per spec "sound off"), a latency calibration screen, lanes, hold notes, a combo multiplier, a "free play" with no pay, request-board cards for Rhythm Grove, play invites that offer it (the spec's "creature play invites can offer it" is not built for Bug Catch either: a play invite is a ball toss (`answerInitiative`, `src/ui/game.ts:410`) and opens no session today), a song picker (the field decides), and a debounce for a double tap.
15. **The browser checks move the page's clock themselves**, as `train-browser.mjs` does for Bug Catch's `requestAnimationFrame` loop (Playwright's `page.clock`, paused and moved only by `runFor`, `train-browser.mjs:246-258`); no clock hook is added to `Game`. That the notes move under `runFor` is not tried for this game: the first browser step of the build is a probe of it.

### Edge cases (a test for each; "(b)" is a browser check)

Song data (`tests/rhythm.test.ts`):
1. Each pattern is 16 characters of `.`, `1`, `2`, `3` and starts with `1`. The three fields differ in tempo and in pattern.
2. The first note of every field at every stage is at 2000 ms; times rise strictly; every time is a whole millisecond; the last is at most 74,000 ms; the gap between kept notes is at least 550 ms for kid and young and at least 250 ms for adult. Literals for Land: the kid notes are at 2000, 2625, 3250, 4500, 5125 ms and the next loop starts at 7000 (eighth 0 of loop 2), and the adult last note is at 73,875.
3. The number of notes per stage is in a range for every field (kid 55 to 85, young 80 to 115, adult 110 to 145); Land's are 73, 102 and 144 (literals); a higher stage has every note of a lower one.
4. `maxScore` is 100 times that count (a literal for one field and stage).

Judging:
5. Boundaries, in `elapsedMs` literals: a tap 80 ms before or after a note is Perfect, 81 ms is Good, 160 ms is Good, 161 ms is a stray and the note is still there. A note whose time is a multiple of 10 is settled as a Miss at the step at `t + 170`: a tap at `t + 160` is Good and a tap at `t + 170` is a stray, the note already a Miss.
6. A stray resets the streak, takes 30 points (the score never below 0), settles no note and counts in `strays`; a second tap on a note already judged is a stray.
7. A note nobody taps is settled as a Miss at the first whole step after `t + goodMs` (not before) and resets the streak; a whole run with no tap ends with every note a Miss, the score 0 and `over`, and no note in flight at the end (Land adult's last note, 73,875, settles at 74,040, before 75,000).
8. Two notes inside one window: a tap goes to the closest; a tie goes to the earlier; the next tap judges the other. Built by hand on a state with notes at 1000 and 1300 ms (the real songs cannot show it: Land's adult notes 6 and 7 are 313 ms apart and the clock moves in 10 ms steps, so a tap within both windows is rare and never a tie): a tap at 1160 goes to the second (140 ms away, against 160), a tap at 1150 is a tie and goes to the first. A play on the real Land adult song covers the usual case: a tap at 4030 judges the note at 3875 (155 ms) and leaves the one at 4188.
9. A judged tap settles its note: it leaves `notes`, is never judged twice, and `tapRhythm` returns its judge and offset. The streak counts hits since the last miss or stray and `best` is its largest; the score is exactly `max(0, 100 * perfects + 60 * goods - 30 * strays)` at every moment, whatever order the events came in.
10. Paused: `stepRhythm` and `tapRhythm` do nothing and the clock does not move; resume goes on where it was; over: no tap counts; a tap before any note is in its window (the first opens at 1840 ms) is a stray.
11. `stepRhythm` fed 7 ms, 16 ms and 100 ms frames reaches the same elapsed time and the same notes in flight for the same total time (up to the frame cap); a stalled frame of 5 s moves the song 100 ms, not 5 s; a non-finite or negative `dtMs` moves it nothing; time fed but under a whole step is carried.
12. `rhythmBandOf` at each line (one point under, on, one over) for each stage, from the integer percentages.
13. A note enters `notes` 1600 ms before its time: the first at 400 ms exactly (and `stepRhythm` says the list changed), the next ones at their time minus 1600.
14. A steady tapper (a tap every 40, 50, ... 700 ms, 20 phases each) never reaches the Silver line on any field at any stage, with the real stray cost (the model is `tests/rhythmBot.ts`'s mash).

Pay and screen (`tests/rhythm-game.test.ts`):
15. `payRhythm` pays Spirit by the tier from the Spirit count (a stat missing from `c.training.sessions` reads 0), leaves the Speed count and Speed alone, honours the stage cap ("Spirit is as high as it can go for now"), adds Discipline and Bond XP by band, writes one log line, and pays nothing for a run with no hit. `payTraining`'s numbers and log text are unchanged (the existing tests stay as they are, but for the one call in edge 19).
16. A creature that left, died or was replaced between the start and the finish pays nobody (`changed()` ends the session when `bornAt` differs: `src/ui/game.ts:637`, already covered for Bug Catch); a second `finishTraining` pays nothing more.
17. `pickTrainGame`: only in phase `ready`; an unknown id changes nothing; the pick survives `closeTraining` within the visit; `startBurst` starts Bug Catch whatever the pick and leaves the pick alone.
18. A song for a form with no field is Land's; for each real field its own.
19. `startLine(game, count)` and `resultLines` for each game and tier (the words "Speed" and "Spirit" from the table, the result line for a no-hit run, the extra rhythm line, "no note" in it).
20. (b) the picker: Bug Catch is pressed first; Rhythm Grove shows its blurb, "Spirit: full reward, 1 of 3 today" and the window numbers; Go begins the game on show; the dialog is labelled with it.
21. (b) the beat: with a frozen clock, a tap at the first note's time (2000 ms into the run) reads Perfect; a tap 100 ms late reads Good, late; a tap with no note near reads "No note, -30" and resets the streak; the expected times are the checker's own literals from the tempo and the pattern, not read from the page. The creature's face carries the hop after a Perfect and a Good and not after a stray or a Miss; under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` it has no animation and the word still shows.

3. **The engine is pure and has no random draw.** `src/game/stack.ts`: `newStack(stage, lengthMs)`, `stepStack(s, dtMs)` (the `step` contract: at most `frameCapMs` a call, whole `stepMs` steps, nothing while paused or over; true when the run ended), `blockX(s)` (the moving block's centre now, a function of `elapsedMs` and the stage only), `dropStack(s)` (judge the drop now; null when paused or over), `stackBandOf(score, stage)`, `scoreOf(s)`. `pause` and `resume` are the shared ones. A session never touches the save and there is no seed. `StackState` is plain data: `stage`, `lengthMs`, `elapsedMs`, `carryMs`, `blocks` (`{ x, w }`, the centre and the width of each block, **the base first**: `blocks[0]` is the base the stack stands on, never trimmed), `w` (the width of the block that is coming), `standMs`, `readyAt` (the elapsed time from which the next drop counts, 0 at the start), `blockPoints`, `perfects`, `goods`, `badDrops` (0, 1 or 2: the forgiven one and the one that toppled), `recovered`, `toppled`, `combo`, `best`, `said` (`{ n, judge, ... }`), `paused`, `over`; a test writes its own `blocks` and `w`. **Height** is `blocks.length - 1`, the blocks placed on the base. **All positions and widths are whole units and every width is even** (`startW` and `growBy` are even; a Good's width is its overlap rounded down to even, and its centre is the overlap's left edge plus half the new width), so every edge is a whole unit and no half unit appears.
4. **The moving block and the landing, on a field `trainTuning.field` (1000) wide.** The block's centre sweeps back and forth between `lo` 150 and `hi` 850 at the stage's speed as a triangle wave of `elapsedMs` (it starts at `lo` going right and runs with the clock, which stops while the run is paused; the period is `2 * (hi - lo) * 1000 / speed` ms, and the position is rounded to a whole unit). The base is `startW` 300 wide with its centre at 500. A drop is judged at `elapsedMs` (the clock the screen shows, within 10 ms of the tap): the block's centre `x` against the top block's centre `cx` and widths `w` (the coming block) and `tw` (the top block); `d = x - cx`; `overlap = max(0, min(x + w/2, cx + tw/2) - max(x - w/2, cx - tw/2))`.
   - **Perfect:** `|d| <= min(perfectU, floor(min(w, tw) / 2))`. The cap on the window keeps a Perfect from ever missing the block below (at least half of the narrower block overlaps) and keeps a very narrow stack playable. The block snaps to `cx` (no animation: it appears aligned, by at most `perfectU` units), keeps its width `w`, the combo goes up by one and, from the `growFrom` 2nd perfect in a row, `w` grows by `growBy` 30, never past `startW`. Points: `perfectPts` 20 plus `comboPts` 5 for each earlier perfect in the combo, at most `comboMax` 5 of them.
   - **Good:** not Perfect, `overlap * 100 >= wobblePct * w` and `overlap >= minW` (`wobblePct` 35, a whole percent so the test has no rounding in it; `minW` 20). The overhang is cut off: the new block is the overlap (even width, as decision 3 says), `w` becomes that width, the combo goes to 0. Points: `goodPts` 10.
   - **After a drop, the next block comes `dropGapMs` later.** A drop that is judged (a Perfect, a Good or a Bad one) sets `readyAt` to `elapsedMs + dropGapMs` (400 `[tune]`); a tap before `readyAt` returns null and changes nothing (no cost, no count), and the moving block is not drawn until then. 400 ms is longer than a Perfect window is wide (about 224 ms, the two sides together), so one crossing of the block gives at most one Perfect, and a double tap does no harm. Without it a tapper could stack a Perfect every 10 ms inside one window with no skill.
   - **Bad:** anything else (little or no overlap). The first one in a run is the wobble recovery: the block is lost, the stack and `w` stay, the combo goes to 0, `recovered` is set, nothing else is lost. The second topples the stack: `toppled`, `over`, the run ends there.
   - After a streak the coming block can be wider than the one below (`w` is `tw` plus `growBy`): a Perfect then puts the wider block at `cx` and it overhangs, by design (the reward for a streak; the picture shows it, and at least half of the narrower block always overlaps). A good run keeps the block wide by landing perfect; a sloppy one trims it a little at a time until a bad drop ends the run, so the skill is the timing of each drop. **At the narrowest width** (`w` and `tw` both `minW`, 20) no Good is possible (a Good needs an overlap of 20, which is the whole block, which is a Perfect), so there only a Perfect holds, within 10 units; the run then ends on the second bad drop. ### 10.2 Decisions

1. **The timing input.** Tapping Attack, Guard or Special does not play the round at once any more (Adult Mode only). It opens a **timing pad** where the three buttons were: a big round pad (at least 96 px) with a ring that shrinks from 2.0 times the target to 1.0 times over `combatTuning.ring.ms` (900 ms, `[tune]`), linear. The tap time is measured with `performance.now()` against the instant the ring meets the target: `offset = tapAt - (startedAt + ring.ms)` (negative is early, positive is late). A pure function `gradeTap(offsetMs)` in a new `src/game/combat/timing.ts` returns `"perfect"` for `|offset| <= perfectMs`, `"good"` for `<= goodMs`, else `"normal"` (so an early or late tap and no tap at all are all `"normal"`; never zero). The windows are `timingWindows`, not copies. If no tap comes by `ring.ms + goodMs + 40` ms (1100 ms after the pad opens, 200 ms after the target, so the latest tap the pad takes is about +200) the pad closes by itself and plays the round as `"normal"`. Any tap while the pad is open, even one 300 ms after it opens (600 ms before the target), is graded by `gradeTap` (`"normal"` that early) and plays the round at once. A pointer tap is taken at `pointerdown`, like the Rhythm and Stack pads; the keyboard (the pad is a focused button) is taken at `keydown` of Space or Enter, not a repeat, because Space fires its `click` on release and a held key would be graded by how long it was held; a `click` with `detail === 0` (assistive technology) is taken at its own time; the first tap resolves the prompt, so a `click` that follows a tap finds no pad.
2. **What a grade does** (`[tune]`; the signature is `playRound(b, choice, chooseFoe = foeChoice, timing: Timing = "normal")`: the chooser stays third, where `tests/combat.test.ts` and `tests/arena.test.ts` pass it today, and `timing` is a new fourth argument that is ignored unless `b.timed`, so a Kid battle given "perfect" plays as "normal"):
   - Attack and Special both: Perfect x1.5 damage, Good x1.2, Normal x1 (after the crit, before the guard). A Perfect **Attack** also gives +1 meter ("Special charges from Perfects"). A Perfect **Special** is x1.5 damage and gives no meter (a Special empties it), so specials do not chain.
   - Guard: Normal factor 0.5 (today's), Good 0.35, **Perfect = the parry**: the foe's blow does 0 damage, and the guard gives +1 extra meter. A parry needs a blow to meet: a Perfect Guard on a round where the foe does not attack or special is an ordinary guard (no bonus, so it cannot be farmed). A parried blow gives no hit-taken meter. The spec's "reflect a small amount" is not built (the meter charge is the spec's other option).
   - The `BattleEvent` gets `timing?: "perfect" | "good"` and `parried?: true`; `arena.ts` `lastHitOn` skips parried blows (no recoil from a parried blow) and `drawStrikes` draws no hit burst for one (a special's spark still flies at the target; the picture is on the contact sheet, looked at, not asserted).
   - A round that sulks gets no grade effect: no x1.5, no parry, no meter bonus (the engine plays it as a sulk and the grade is dropped).
3. **The foe's move is shown first (the telegraph).** A timed guard only means something if the player can see the blow coming. A battle has `timed: boolean` (false unless `beginFight` is given `timed`; `startFight` passes `save.settings.adultMode === true`) and `foeNext?: BattleAction`. `planFoe(b, chooseFoe = foeChoice)` (new, in `battle.ts`) draws the foe's next choice from the battle's own random state. `beginFight(save, c, nowMs, timed = false)` calls it once with the default chooser when `timed` (`startFight` passes `save.settings.adultMode === true`); `playRound` calls it again with its own `chooseFoe` and its own random generator after the blows of every round that does not end the fight (`if (b.timed && !b.over) b.foeNext = chooseFoe(b, rng)` before the state is saved), and uses `foeNext` instead of drawing when it is set. So a custom chooser reaches every plan but the first; a test that wants one for the first calls `planFoe(b, chooser)` itself. `newBattle` gets a last parameter `timed = false`. A Kid battle never sets it, so Kid's random numbers and fights are the same as before. The screen reads `arena.battle.timed`, not the setting, so changing the mode in the middle of a fight changes nothing.
4. **The sulk (Adult battles only).** The roll still happens every round (same random numbers); a sulk only takes effect if `b.sulks < 1`, so a creature sulks at most once per fight (`Battle.sulks` counts). The note is `Sulking: discipline too low. Training helps.` through `game.say` (the spec's dash replaced with a colon, the project writes no em dashes), so the failed tap is never silent.
5. **Feedback words** (through `game.say`, one note per round, Adult only): `Perfect attack`, `Good attack`, `Perfect guard` (only when it parried), `Good guard`, `Perfect special`, `Good special`; a tap outside the good window says `Early` or `Late`; no tap says nothing. A sulk's note wins over these.

### 10.3 Edge cases (before the code; each gets a test or a browser check)

1. `gradeTap` at every whole millisecond from -200 to +200: 80 and -80 perfect, 81 and -81 good, 160 and -160 good, 161 and -161 normal; `NaN`, `Infinity`, `-Infinity` and `undefined` are normal.
2. The default `timing` is `"normal"` and a battle that is not `timed` ignores it: every existing call returns what it returned before. The proof is the golden record already in the branch (`tests/combatGolden.ts`, `tests/fixtures/combat-golden.json`, `tests/combat-golden.test.ts`, written from the untouched engine at 6408759: 12 fights round by round, 15 starts at every rung of three stages, 12 finished fights with what they pay and change). The same seed and the same scripted choices with `"normal"`, `"good"` and `"perfect"` use the same random numbers in every round in which both fights go on (the rolls are drawn whatever the grade). The round that ends a fight can end in a different random state: a knocked-out foe does not strike, so it draws fewer numbers. The test compares the state only up to that round.
4. Damage, each on fixed numbers: a Good Attack is exactly `round(normal * 1.2)` and a Perfect Attack `round(normal * 1.5)` for the same roll, and the same for a Special; with a crit, and with the target guarding; a Good guard takes 0.35 of the blow and a Normal guard 0.5; a Good or Normal guard on a 1-damage blow leaves 1 (only a Perfect parry reaches 0).
5. A parry: foe attack or special parried gives 0 damage and no hit-taken meter, the guard's own +1 and the Perfect's +1 (capped at `meterMax`); no parry when the foe guards (nothing to meet): a Perfect guard against a foe that guards gains exactly the Normal guard's +1 meter, and the meter is asserted; a parried special still empties the foe's meter.

### 14.2 Decisions (mine, each changeable)

1. **A recipe is read off the rules, never stored.** `src/game/recipes.ts` (pure, every word in it) answers `recipeOf(formId)`: one step per form of `lineageOf`, each with the form it produces, the stage word and a text built from `tuning.growth` and the field's plan. The first step is "Hatch an egg." A kid's step: "Baby: spend 60 awake minutes in the Water (4 cells of 15 minutes) before any other field gets 4. After 6 hours, the field with the most cells wins." A young's step: "Kid: feed it mostly Seafood. After 24 hours, the food with the most cells wins (2 servings make a cell)." The fourth young: "Kid: feed it pellets and treats only, or keep the top foods tied. After 24 hours, that tie gives this one." An adult's step, by tier: 1 "Young: after 48 hours, with 5 hunger cells, 5 happy cells and no mistakes. Add no mistake in the baby and kid stages for a Perfect star."; 2 "...with 1 mistake, or no mistake but fewer than 5 hunger or happy cells."; 3 "...with 2 to 5 mistakes."; 4 "...with 6 or more mistakes." The special: "Leave a baby or a kid with no care for 4 awake hours. A meal, clean-up, medicine, pet or tickle counts as care." The words name no creature, only fields, foods and stages, so an unraised creature stays "???" (its parents' names are never printed).
2. **Where it shows (Adult Mode).** In the Book a cell becomes a button (`aria-pressed`), one selected at a time. The selected cell's card opens directly under its row (so it is under the finger, not off screen at the top of a long panel): the face or the dark shape, the name or "Not raised yet", "Perfect star" when it has one, and the steps as a numbered list, each marked "Done" when the form it produces is in the Book. A "Close" button in the card, and a second tap on the cell, shut it and put focus back on the cell. Adult cells drop the one-line hint (the card says it better and the line did not fit a quarter-width cell); Kid cells keep it.
3. **"Next to find" (the spec's acceptance line: "state, from the Book alone, the exact recipe for their next unearned creature").** One line at the top of the Book: the first unraised form, in Book order, whose parent is raised, as "Next to find: a kid in the Water" or "Next to find: a young in the Water" or "the special form", then its last step's text. When every form is raised: "Every creature is raised."
4. **What a perfect run is (the owner's answer 4 of 2026-11-22, decisions.md).** A creature that grows up into a tier 1 adult by natural growth with no care mistake in any stage. Tier 1 already needs 0 mistakes in the young stage, so the new condition is 0 mistakes in the baby and kid stages. Judged once, at the moment it grows up (`evolveIfReady`, not `evolveTo`, so a dev jump awards nothing), and kept: a later mistake as an adult changes nothing ("permanently" in the spec, and rewards only go up).
5. **The saved facts (all optional or defaulted, no version change).** `Creature.pastMistakes?: number` (the mistakes of the stages already finished; `evolveTo` adds the old `counters.careMistakes` to it before the counters are replaced), `Creature.perfect?: true`, `Save.perfect: string[]` (the form ids raised as a perfect run, no duplicates: the Book's star, which must outlive the creature, since a dead creature is dropped when the next egg starts), `RetiredLife.perfect?: true` (copied at retire, so the gallery keeps the star and A4.5 can read it). A creature with no `pastMistakes` counts its earlier stages as clean (the benefit of the doubt, so no old save is judged on what it never recorded). Lifetime mistakes = `pastMistakes + counters.careMistakes`, read through one function that treats a missing, negative or non-number value as 0.
6. **The Forecast chases it live.** `forecast()` gets `perfect: { kind: "on" | "out" | "kept"; text: string } | null`: a baby, kid or young with 0 lifetime mistakes: "Perfect run: still possible. No mistake so far in this life."; with some: "Perfect run: out of reach for this life (2 mistakes so far). It grows the same, and every creature still counts toward the Book."; an adult with the star: "Perfect run. It grew up with no mistake, and the Book keeps its star."; an adult without it and the special: nothing at all (a creature is never labelled as short of it). The lead card shows the star beside a perfect adult's name.
7. **The star in the Book.** A small star in the corner of a cell whose form is in `save.perfect`, and in the card; the line at the top gets ", P with a Perfect star" only when P is at least 1. The completed lives list shows the star beside a perfect life (Adult Mode only). New icon `star` (8 x 8, one more name and one drawing).
8. **Copy rule.** No word of loss or failure anywhere: the `retire.test.ts` banned list (`sacrifice release lose lost delete die death forever gone`) plus `fail failed failure loss wrong bad poor worse ruined miss missed`, checked over every string for every form. "Mistake" stays: it is the game's own word for a call left unanswered, and the spec's own recipe example uses it.
9. **No new tuning numbers.** Every number in a recipe comes from `tuning.growth`, so changing the tuning changes the words (the test pins the literals, so it must change with them).
10. **Kid Mode.** `BookPanel` reads `isAdultMode(save)` once; with it off the markup is the one it has today (an `<li>` with the hint, no button, no star, no line, no card). `adultTier` moves from `forecast.ts` into `growth.ts` (it reads only `data/fields`, and `sim.ts` already imports `growth.ts`); `forecast.ts` and the forecast test import it from there.

### 14.3 Edge cases (before the code; each gets a test or a browser check, "(b)" is a browser check)

1. Every one of the 65 forms has a recipe whose steps are `lineageOf` in order, whose last step produces the form, and which is `null` for an unknown id (and for "constructor" and "toString", whose lookups must be own-key checks, as `formOf` does).
2. Following a recipe grows the form (the property): for each form a test builds a baby, gives it exactly what each step names (its field minutes, its food servings, its mistakes and cells), and asks `nextForm`, one stage after another, whether the creature becomes the lineage's next form. Tier 2 is tried at (0 mistakes, 4 hunger cells) and (1 mistake, full cells), tier 3 at 2 and 5 mistakes, tier 4 at 6 and 99, tier 1 at (0, full); the fourth young by pellets only and by a tie.
3. The words are pinned as literals for a sample (the Water kid, a young by food, the fourth young, one adult of each tier, the special, the first step), and a test says the numbers come from the tuning (changing `tier2MaxMistakes` changes the tier 2 and 3 lines).
4. `nextToFind`: with only the baby raised it is the first kid in Book order; with a kid raised it is the next in book order whose parent is raised; with all 65 raised it is null; a `found` set with a bad id changes nothing.
5. Perfect, by natural growth: 0 lifetime mistakes and a tier 1 form is perfect (the flag, `save.perfect`, a log line); 1 mistake as a baby, then none, a tier 1 adult is not; 0 mistakes with 4 hunger cells (a tier 2 adult) is not; two mistakes spread over the baby and kid stages add up; the special is never perfect; a dev `evolveTo` into a tier 1 adult awards nothing but still adds the old stage's mistakes; the same form raised perfect twice has one entry in `save.perfect`.
6. A perfect adult that makes mistakes keeps the star; it retires with `perfect: true` on its life; a new egg clears `creature.perfect` and leaves `save.perfect` and the life as they are.
7. The loader: a save with no `perfect` gets []; a non-array, a non-string, an unknown id and a duplicate are dropped; a retired record keeps `perfect` only when it is exactly `true`; a creature's `pastMistakes` of -3, NaN, "2" or null counts as 0 and a `perfect` that is not `true` is ignored; the loader never throws. A save written by an older build and loaded now has the star lists empty, and no creature or Book entry changes.
8. Cloud: the new fields travel in the whole save, as every field does (newest wins). An older build (read from `migrateSave`, not tried: no older build is run) passes the top-level `perfect` and the creature's `pastMistakes` and `perfect` through, as it does `bond` and `training` (known-issues.md, the `bond` and `training` entries), but its `retired` filter rebuilds each record from three fields, so it drops a life's `perfect` on its next save. Recorded in known-issues.md, not fixed.
9. The Forecast line is live: a mistake taken now (a what-if is not a mistake) turns "still possible" into "out of reach" at the next second; a baby that never made one reads "still possible"; an adult with the star reads the kept line; an adult without it and the special read nothing.
10. (b) Layout at 320, 360 and 390 wide, ratio 1 and 2, and 740 x 360 and 844 x 390 on their side: no sideways scroll, no element past the window's width inside the panel, every cell button at least 44 px by 44 px, the card's text measured with a Range against its box, the star not on the face (rects), the card under the selected cell's row, never past the page's end.
11. (b) Keys and focus: Tab reaches each cell; Enter and Space open the card; focus stays on the cell on open; Close and a second tap close it and focus returns to the cell; only one card is open; the card is a `role="group"` named for the creature (the name or "Not raised yet"); a cell's `aria-label` is "Name, raised" or "Not raised yet" (+ ", Perfect star"), and for an unraised form it never contains the creature's name.
12. (b) The text on screen (read, not the label): the card's steps as literal strings for six forms in a browser, "Done" marks follow what is raised, the Next line, the count line with and without a star, the star beside a perfect life in the list.
13. (b) Kid Mode: no button in any cell, no star, no Next line, no card, the hint still there; Kid compare 0 differences against `origin/main` (with its self-test).
14. (b) A reload keeps the stars; a Book open while the creature grows up shows the new star at the next render.
15. (b) A save whose `save.perfect` holds a form id that is not in the Book (a bad file) shows no star on any cell and does not break the count.
