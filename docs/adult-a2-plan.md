## 2. What is true today

- **Coins and materials are earned and never spent.** Every write is in `game/habitat/jobs.ts` (`stepJob`: a job's haul) and `game/combat/fight.ts` (a won fight: coins, and one material half the time). Every read is a display (`ui/HabitatBar.tsx`, `ui/AdultHud.tsx`) or `sync/sync.ts` `isUntouched` (an egg save that has not been touched counts as untouched: it also needs 0 coins, an empty bag, no hauls, no creature and at most one log line). `game/crafting/` holds only a README.
- **The five materials** (`data/habitat.ts`): Fiber, Herbs, Wood, Fish, Stone, dropped by Bug meadow, Herb patch, Trees, Pond and Rock pile. There is no cap on the bag.
- **The spots** (`data/habitat.ts`, `spots`), in minutes / energy / coins / material:

  | Spot | From | Min | Energy | Coins | Material | Coins per energy (computed) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Bug meadow (fiber) | baby | 3 | 5 | 1 to 2 | 1 to 2 | 0.300 |
  | Herb patch (herbs) | baby | 3 | 5 | 1 to 2 | 1 to 2 | 0.300 |
  | Trees (wood) | young | 5 | 10 | 2 to 3 | 1 to 2 | 0.250 |
  | Pond (fish) | young | 6 | 10 | 2 to 4 | 1 to 2 | 0.300 |
  | Rock pile (stone) | adult | 8 | 15 | 3 to 5 | 1 to 3 | 0.267 |

  A creature only sets off if it keeps at least 20 energy (`habitatTuning.energyFloor`). So one full bar (100 down to 20) is worth, on average, 16 meadow or herb jobs (24 coins, 24 materials, 48 minutes), 8 trees jobs (20 coins, 12 materials), 8 pond jobs (24 coins, 12 materials) or 5 rock jobs (20 coins, 10 materials). Energy returns at 0.25 per minute asleep (`tuning.sleepEnergyPerMinute`, 15 an hour; refilling 80 energy takes 5 h 20 min) and falls 100 to 0 over 16 awake hours. **Coins are the scarce thing (about 20 to 24 a bar) and materials are plentiful (10 to 24 a bar).**
- **`refusal` order for a job** (`jobRefusal`): busy, asleep, sick, too-young, tired. In Adult Mode a refused spot is `aria-disabled` and a tap shows the reason as a short note over the picture (`game.whyNot` and `game.say` in `ui/game.ts`, shown by `ui/Stage.tsx`; the words are `game/reasons.ts` `spotReason`); in Kid Mode it is a small icon. Adult Mode already writes a short word under each spot and the stage on a locked one (A1.3).
- **While away**, the adult context line already reads "Away at <Spot> · <back text>" (`game/hud.ts`). (Written before A2.1: A2.1, built on branch `adult-a2` (merged as PR #31), has since added the job card with a progress bar and a haul preview in Adult Mode; A2.2 branches from it, as `adult-a2-2`.)
- **Call back** (`actions.ts` `callBack`) ended the job and brought **nothing** back when this was written; A2.1 made it keep what the time earned (`callBackHaul`), energy still not returned. The Kid Mode button is still a house icon with no words (`HabitatBar.tsx`).
- **The haul** is a modal "Welcome back" card (`ui/HaulCard.tsx`) that shows the oldest of `save.hauls` (at most 5, `habitatTuning.unseenHaulsMax`) and is dismissed by a tap (`dismissHaul`). So a finished job cannot sit unseen behind a badge: the card already stops the player until it is read. The spec's nav badge and "pile in the scene" are therefore not needed for honesty and are left out (section 3, item 7).
- **There is no furniture, no cosmetics and no owned-items state.** The room is one fixed drawing (`art/room.ts`: wall, floor, window, rug, bowl, bed, in plain rectangles). `Save` has no `owned`, `requests` or `placed`.
- **Saves:** version 3; a new field is added with a default in `migrateSave` (as `book` and `retired` were); `isSave` checks only a few fields; the server stops at 256,000 bytes and a request under 60,000 characters is sent with `keepalive` (`sync/cloud.ts`).
- **Panels** open from `ui/App.tsx` (`panel`: settings, dev, book, forecast), from buttons in the Adult header's strip (`ui/AdultHud.tsx`).
- **Foods:** unlimited (owner's answer 1). `feed` (`actions.ts`) calls `countServing` and `countCareCells`; growth counts only a field's three foods (`growth.ts`).

## 3. Decisions (mine, changeable)

1. **Shop and board are Adult Mode only** (they sit behind `settings.adultMode`, as the spec's Spec 1 says adult features do). What a player owns still works if the mode is switched off, and nothing is lost either way. A profile that has never bought anything sees no change at all, and Kid Mode's own screens (room, header, habitat, arena) are not changed by A2.2 to A2.4; a profile that bought a wallpaper or a bed and later switches Adult Mode off keeps their look and effect, on purpose (taking them away would be a penalty). The Kid Mode comparison therefore runs on Kid saves that own nothing. See Q2.
2. **The spec's catalogue is cut to what can be built without later specs** (section 5): no Toy Shelf (needs Bond, A3), no Lamp (needs field pickups, A3), no Garden Plot (needs the garden timer, A3), no Storage Box (the bag has no cap, so there is nothing to raise), no Job Board+ (a creature has one job at a time; a second slot has no meaning yet). That leaves 13 items against the spec's 12 to 16.
3. **Furniture has a fixed place** in the room (a bed spot, a bowl spot, a wall spot), not free placement. The spec asks for free placement as "the first slice of the room editor", which is a whole piece of work (drag, snap, rotate, an invalid-spot outline) for two furniture items. See Q3.
4. **No coins for real money, no rotation, no timers, nothing expires** (the spec's guardrails apply to every slice).
5. **Prices are set from the measured income, not guessed.** Section 2 gives the arithmetic (about 20 to 24 coins per energy bar). Before A2.2 is built a short script will run the real simulation for a day of play and record coins and materials, and the prices will be written from that, with the spec's tiers (a cosmetic about 1 day, a useful item 2 to 3 days, an upgrade about a week) and marked `[tune]`.
6. **Every material has at least two uses in the catalogue** (the spec's rule): section 5 spreads the five materials over the items so each appears in at least two costs. A material used by fewer than two items would stop dropping instead (not planned: it would change the habitat).
7. **Left out of A2.1 although the spec lists them:** the Habitat nav badge and the haul pile in the scene (the modal card already does their job, section 2), a coin cost on a spot (spots unlock by stage, decisions.md), a "second job slot".
8. **Call back keeps what the time earned**, rounded down, exactly and shown before the tap (section 6). The energy it spent is still not returned, as today (the spec's "never a penalty" is read as: nothing the creature already has is taken away; the energy is spent either way). See Q1.

## 4. Questions for the owner (the plan stops here)

- **Q1. Call back, in both modes?** Today it brings nothing back. Planned: it keeps `floor(average haul x fraction of the time done)` of each of coins and material, shown on the button in Adult Mode ("Call back now: 1 coin, 1 Fish"), and Kid Mode's house icon does the same without words (and a call back that pays shows the Welcome back card, which is a new sight in Kid Mode). It is the one rule change that also touches Kid Mode's play (not its other screens). The spec says "never zero", which would let a player send and recall at once for a free coin, so it is not copied: an early call back keeps nothing, and the energy it spent is not given back (it is spent either way). *My pick: both modes, proportional, rounded down.*
- **Q2. Shop and board in Adult Mode only?** The alternative is both modes (a Kid Mode game would gain a shop button and cards to read, against CLAUDE.md 7.10). *My pick: Adult Mode only.*
- **Q3. Fixed furniture places, or free placement?** *My pick: fixed places now; free placement waits for the real room editor.*
- **Q4. Is the cut catalogue (section 5) right,** and are hats a separate slice that may slip? (A hat must sit on 65 different creatures in every pose.) The shop has nine items after A2.2, below the spec's 12 to 16, until hats land; if hats are dropped, the gap would be filled by cheap decor drawn in the room (a rug, a plant, a poster) rather than by more hats. *My pick: hats last, and drop them if they look wrong.*
- **Q5. Adults: one tap to look at a spot, a second to send?** The spec wants a card on tap with a Send button; today one tap sends. *My pick: the spec's two taps in Adult Mode (a mistaken send costs energy), one tap in Kid Mode as now.*
- **Q6. Which slice first?** *My pick: A2.1, because it needs no new saved state and the shop's wording ("you have 4, you need 2") reuses its cards.*

## 6. A2.1 in detail: spot cards, the job card, an honest call back

### New and changed

- **A spot card** (Adult Mode only). A tap on a spot selects it and shows a card under the spot row: the spot's name and what it gives ("Pond: Fish"), "6 min, 10 energy", "Haul: 2 to 4 coins, 1 to 2 Fish" (ranges from `data/habitat.ts`, never invented), and either a **Send** button or the reason it cannot go (the existing `spotReason` strings, plus a line "it has 61, it needs 30 to set off" for energy: `energyNeededForSpot` already exists). A second tap on the same spot or on Send sends. A locked spot's card says "opens at Young" and the stage the creature is now. Kid Mode keeps one tap to send.
- **The job card** (Adult Mode, while the creature is away, in the place the spot card takes): the spot, a progress bar (elapsed over total), the energy it spent, "Haul: 2 to 4 coins, 1 to 2 Fish", and the **Call back** button with its words. The adult context line stays as it is.
- **An honest call back:** `callBack(save, nowMs)` keeps its result type (`"called" | "nothing"`). It ends the job and adds the haul the time earned, given by a helper: `fraction = clamp((nowMs - startedAt) / (endsAt - startedAt), 0, 1)`; coins and material each `Math.floor(average of the range x fraction)`. That haul is added to the bag and pushed as a normal haul (so the "Welcome back" card shows it) when coins plus material is more than zero; `ui/HaulCard.tsx` will not draw a row that is 0 (a call back can pay a coin and no material), which no full haul ever hits. A call back that earned nothing only ends the job, as today. The energy is not returned, as today (the button says so).
- **One helper, two users:** `callBackHaul(job, nowMs)` in `game/habitat/jobs.ts` (it takes the spot, so it knows the ranges) is read by both the action and the button's words, so the label always equals what the tap does (the A1.3 rule, tested by a sweep).
- **Words** live in one pure module (`game/habitat/cards.ts`), like `game/retire.ts`: no word of loss ("you lose"), and a test over the whole strings.
- **No new saved state, no migration.** Selecting a spot is panel-local state.

### Edge cases (a test for each; "(b)" means a browser check)

Rules (unit, on caught-up saves; the screen list starts at 15):
1. `callBackHaul` at 0%, 25%, 33%, 50%, 99% and 100% of a job, for each of the five spots, equals the literal expected numbers (computed by hand from the ranges, written as literals, rounded down). The cases include an exact whole number (Rock pile coins, average 4, at 25% is exactly 1), an exact half (Bug meadow coins, average 1.5, at one third is exactly 0.5 and pays 0) and a value just under a whole number.
2. A clock set back so `now` is before `startedAt`: fraction 0, no haul, no negative number. A clock far ahead (job already past `endsAt`): the simulation has already finished the job, so `callBack` returns "nothing", as today.
3. `callBack` on a creature that is not away returns "nothing" and changes nothing; on a dead or egg save returns "nothing".
4. The haul is added once: coins and material in the bag match the haul pushed; calling twice adds nothing the second time.
5. Energy is not returned (`stats.energy` the same before and after).
6. A call back worth nothing pushes no haul (no empty "Welcome back" card) and logs "Called back from the <Spot> early"; one worth a coin and no material pushes a haul with `amount` 0, and the card draws only the coin row.
7. The sweep: for every spot and every fraction step of 5%, the number the button's words state equals what `callBack` then adds.
8. A job that ends in the same moment as the tap: the simulation finishes it first (the action catches up), so the full haul arrives and `callBack` returns "nothing".
9. The cards' words: every string for every spot, the banned-word test, the numbers are the data's own (a literal check against `data/habitat.ts`).
10. `jobRefusal` is unchanged (the existing tests pass untouched).
11. Night falls while the creature is away: a job is not stopped by sleep (`stepJob` never asks), so it finishes as today; a call back while it is asleep away works the same as awake (`callBack` has no sleep or sick check). A test fixes this as today's behaviour, so a change to it is a decision.
12. The creature gets sick while away: the job finishes as today; a call back works. It dies while away (phase `dead`): `callBack` returns "nothing", no haul, no coins, and the habitat bar and cards are not shown (`HabitatBar` returns null for a creature that is not alive).
13. An away adult cannot retire (A1.4 refuses "away"), so a call back never meets a retired creature; the test asserts the order: retire refuses, call back works, then retire works.
14. The passive device: a call back adds coins to the save, and the save is one document that syncs by the existing newest-save-wins rule. A device left open with the old save (job still running, no call back coins) that saves later brings the job back and removes the coins: the call back is undone, there is no double pay (that device's job finishes with a full haul on its own clock), and the rule is the same one that known-issues.md records for Retire. A test builds both saves and checks the coin total of each, and the entry in known-issues.md is added with the slice.

Screen (b), real clicks, Adult Mode, at 320, 360 and 390 wide (pixel ratio 1 and 2):
15. (b) A tap on a spot shows its card with the literal numbers; a second tap sends; a refused spot shows its reason and no Send button; a locked spot shows the stage word.
16. (b) While away, the job card shows the progress and the call back words; moving the frozen clock changes the progress and the words; tapping Call back adds the shown haul to the saved game.
17. (b) Kid Mode: one tap sends and the house icon calls back, with the same haul rule; no card and no words appear.
18. (b) Every line of each card fits (a Range against its box), the panel does not scroll sideways, buttons are at least 44 px high, and each button has its own fill and edge (the `btn-look` check, from the A1.4 tooling).
19. (b) Two quick taps on Call back: one haul. (b) A Feed tray open while the creature goes away: unchanged from today.
20. (b) The Kid Mode comparison with `origin/main` shows 0 differences (the habitat page is in it, so a stray change to Kid Mode's spot row would show). Its saves have no hauls, so the new Kid Mode haul card of a paying call back is covered by 17, not by this.

Mutation targets (break, watch fail on its own assertion, restore, diff): the fraction (no clamp), the rounding (nearest or up instead of down), the average, the coins/material swap, energy returned, a haul pushed when zero, the card's numbers from the wrong spot, the Kid Mode branch (the card showing in Kid Mode), the second-tap rule, the label not equal to the action.

## 9. A2.2a in detail: the shop, four items (written 2026-11-23, before any code)

**Why two pull requests.** Items 1 to 4 are things the room shows and the care rules read; items 5 to 9 are foods, which touch the feed tray, the menu, `countServing` and the growth counts, a different set of edge cases. A2.2a ships the whole spend loop (wallet, owned, undo, panel, previews) with the four items that need no food rules; A2.2b adds the five foods to a shop that already works. Until A2.2b lands, Fish has no use in the shop (section 3, decision 6 is met by the whole catalogue, not by A2.2a alone).

**Foods correction (settled here, for A2.2b).** The table in section 5 says items 5 to 7 are "a new food id each". decisions.md, answer 1 (the owner's, closed) says shop foods are boosters of the existing food types and none is a new type. decisions.md wins: A2.2b adds no `FoodId`.

**Prices come from the measurement** (`tools/verify/run-income.sh`, 14 simulated days of an adult, jobs only, days 2 to 14; fights pay a little more): a careful player (looks in every 5 minutes) earns 18.9 coins and 13.9 materials a day going round the five spots (20.4 coins at the best spot alone); three 15-minute visits a day earn 12.2 coins and 8.2 materials; two visits (08:00 and 19:00) earn 7.7 coins and 5.4 materials. A player who visits only at 08:00 loses the creature on day 3; one who visits only at 19:00 keeps it alive but earns nothing, because the energy has run down by then (the creature wakes at 100 and falls 6.25 an hour awake, so every job is refused as tired). Both rows are printed by the script. Materials: the three-visit player gets 1.5 to 1.9 of each material a day, so Comfy Bed (3 Fiber, 2 Wood) is about 2 days of Fiber and 1.3 days of Wood, and the mat (3 Fiber, 2 Herbs) about 2 and 1.3: the material side stays inside the coin side's 2 to 3 days. **A "day" in the spec's tiers is the three-visit player's 12 coins.** The prices, all `[tune]`: Wallpaper: Meadow 12 coins + 2 Herbs; Wallpaper: Dusk 12 coins + 2 Stone; Feeding Mat 25 coins + 3 Fiber + 2 Herbs; Comfy Bed 35 coins + 3 Fiber + 2 Wood. The section 5 drafts (60, 50, 30, 30) were about 5, 4 and 2.5 days for that player.

**What each item does** (`data/shop.ts` holds the numbers; `game/shop.ts` the rules):
- **Comfy Bed:** while the creature sleeps at home (night or nap, and not away on a job: `updateSleep` sets `sleep` to `night` even for a creature out working, and that one is not in its bed), happiness falls half as fast as it does now (`tuning.shop.bedHappinessFactor` 0.5, times the asleep rate that `asleepDecayFactor` gives). Nothing else changes. It is drawn in the room (a bigger quilt and a second pillow on the bed).
- **Feeding Mat:** a meal counts as a hunger cell (young stage) when hunger was under 95 before it, rose, and ended at **90 or more** (`tuning.shop.matHungerGrace` 5) instead of 95 or more. It never counts less than without the mat. A meal that did not raise hunger counts nothing (a pet at hunger 92 must not count: `touch` calls `countCareCells` with an unchanged hunger, so the rule gets a "rose" check). The refusal line (95) is unchanged. The Growth Forecast's hunger-cell note says 90 when the mat is owned. It is drawn in the room under the bowl.
- **Wallpaper: Meadow / Dusk:** recolour the wall. `wall` names the one shown (null: the original). Buying a wallpaper shows it; "Use" and "Stop using" on an owned wallpaper switch.

**New saved state** (all with a default in `migrateSave`, as `retired` was): `owned: string[]` (known ids, no duplicates, in the order bought), `wall: string | null` (an owned wallpaper or null), `lastBuy: { item: string; at: number; wall: string | null } | null` (the one purchase that can still be undone; `wall` is what was shown before it). About 100 bytes. The save stays version 3.

**Rules** (`game/shop.ts`):
- `buy(save, id, nowMs)`: catches the save up first (a job that ends before now pays first), then refuses in this order: `unknown`, `owned`, `poor` (coins or any material short); otherwise spends coins and each material in one step, adds the id to `owned`, sets `wall` if it is a wallpaper, sets `lastBuy`, logs `Bought <name>`. A refusal changes nothing except what the catch-up itself did (the buy catches up first, so a test copies the save after `simulateTo` at the same moment, or uses `nowMs` equal to `lastSimulatedAt`, and compares against that copy).
- `undoBuy(save, nowMs)`: catches up, then if `lastBuy` exists and `0 <= nowMs - lastBuy.at <= 10000` it gives back exactly the price, removes the item, restores `wall` if the item was the wall shown, clears `lastBuy` and logs `Undid <name>`; otherwise it returns `late` (and clears `lastBuy`) or `none`. A clock set back (negative time since) is `late`, so the window is never longer than 10 seconds.
- `useWall(save, id | null)`: only an owned wallpaper or null; returns whether it changed.
- Everything works in any phase (a retired adult has coins and an egg).
- The shop is the fourth button of the nav in Adult Mode ("Built differently" below) and nowhere in Kid Mode. What a player owns works in Kid Mode too (decision 1).

**Where the new inputs go (every caller, from a grep; review finding list in progress.md).** The owned list is `save.owned`; a helper `game/perks.ts` turns it into the two numbers (`bedFactor(owned)`, `hungerGrace(owned)`), so no screen or rule reads item ids itself.
- **The simulation:** `statChangePerMinute(c, stat, asleep, owned = none)` (care.ts:60) is called by `decayStats` (care.ts:74), which `stepCare(save, ...)` calls (care.ts:20, from sim.ts:55): `stepCare` passes `save.owned` into `decayStats`. Without this the bed would show in the HUD and never act in play.

## 10. A2.2b in detail: the five foods (written 2026-11-23, before any code; branch `adult-a2-2b` off main 2f4bc0b)

**What a shop food is (my design, changeable; the one decision this slice rests on).** Section 5 says "a food whose one meal", "unlocked once", decisions.md answer 1 says "Shop foods are optional boosters of the existing food types" and "none is a new food type", and section 9 says no new `FoodId`. So a shop food is a **dish**: an item in the shop (`kind: "food"`) that, once owned, adds one button to a second row of the Feed tray. A dish meal is a meal of one of the creature's own field foods (the same hunger, happiness, wellness and digest numbers as that food, `data/foods.ts`) that **counts differently toward the kid's food cells** (`growth.ts` `countServing`; only the kid stage counts servings, and `pickYoung` counts only a field's three foods). Opt in per meal, never automatic: a plain meal still counts 1, so owning a dish can never make anything worse (the first idea, a passive perk like the bed and the mat, was dropped because a recipe that adds a second serving would turn "feed only meat" into a tie, which gives the fourth young, and the player could not avoid it). Not chosen: five new tray buttons in the one row (the tray is one row of at most five equal columns, `app.css:272`; ten would halve every button, and the figure is measured on the mock before the build, not guessed).

**The three kinds.** A field's three foods are, in `plan.foods` order, filling, light and sweet (checked in `data/foods.ts`: meat, seafood and chicken give 35 hunger; carrot, seaweed and corn 20 hunger and 3 wellness; worm, shrimp and syrup 25 hunger and 5 happiness, in Land, Water and Sky). Dishes name a kind by that position (0, 1, 2), so one dish works in every field and no per-field data is added.

**The five items** (prices `[tune]`, from the measured day: the three-visit player earns 12.2 coins and 8.2 materials, 1.5 to 1.9 of each material (section 9); section 5's drafts were 40 and 50 coins; the bed (3 days) and mat (2 days) are priced the same way):

### What it is

Three requests on a board, each a single action the creature can do today: feed it a named food twice, spend 20 minutes in a named field, finish a job at a named spot, win a fight, buy a named item. Each pays coins when it is done, and every fourth one drawn also pays an item the player does not own yet (shown on the card from the start). A finished request is paid at once, its slot says what it paid, and a new request is drawn 15 minutes later. Nothing expires. The board is a second tab in the Shop panel, in Adult Mode only.

### Checked in the code (2026-11-24)

- **`Save` has no board field.** `migrateSave` (`game/state.ts`) gives a missing field its default, as `retired` and `owned` do; `isUntouched` (`sync/sync.ts`) lists the fields that make a save "touched" (`owned` and `lastBuy` among them).
- **The simulation is a loop of whole minutes** (`sim.ts` `simulateTo` calling `stepMinute`); `stepMinute` runs `stepCare`, then `stepJob`, then the growth counters, only while the creature is alive. The same code runs while the app is open and for time the app was closed. A dead creature stops the loop (the rest of the time is skipped).
- **The events that exist:** a meal (`actions.ts` `feed`, result "ate"; a dish meal is a meal of the creature's own field food, `dishes.ts`), a minute at a place (`creature.place`; `growth.ts` `countFieldMinute` counts it for a baby only, so a request needs its own count), a finished job (`jobs.ts` `stepJob`; `endJobEarly` is a call back and is not a chore), a won fight (`fight.ts` `finishFight`), and a purchase (`shop.ts` `buy`, with the 10 second undo in `undoBuy`).
- **What a creature can do depends on its stage:** the menu is pellets, the treat and (once it has a field) the field's three foods (`growth.ts` `menuOf`); a spot opens by stage (`data/habitat.ts`: Bug meadow and Herb patch from baby, Trees and Pond from young, Rock pile from adult); a fight needs a kid (`combatTuning.minStage`, `data/combat.ts`); any open field can be visited at any stage.
- **The Shop panel** (`ui/ShopPanel.tsx`) is a dialog named "Shop" with a wallet row, a room preview and a list; it opens from the fourth Adult Mode nav button (`ui/Nav.tsx`).
- **A fifth nav button does not fit.** Measured on a mock with the real `app.css` (kept as `tools/verify/nav-mock.mjs`): the four-button row at 320 wide gives each button 66 px and "Habitat" needs 46.2 of 62; with five buttons each is 51.2 px and "Habitat" needs 46.2 of 47, a 0.8 px margin that a phone's own font would break. "Requests" (57.5 px) cannot fit at all. So the board is not a nav button.
- **A note over the room** exists (`Game.say`, Adult Mode only, never through `changed()`), the way a button says why it did nothing.
- **Both modes' time and randomness:** the simulation's random numbers come from `save.rngState`, and a Kid Mode profile that has never used Adult Mode must see no change, so the board must not consume `rngState` and must do nothing when Adult Mode is off.

### Decisions (mine, changeable)

1. **The board is a second tab of the Shop panel** (tabs "Items" and "Requests"; "Items" is the default, so every A2.2 screen is as it was). The nav row stays four buttons. See Q1.
2. **Five request types** (the ones that exist today), all single actions at every stage: `feed` (a named food, 2 meals), `field` (a named field, 20 minutes), `job` (a named spot, 1 finished job), `fight` (win 1), `buy` (a named item, 1). The spec's chained adult requests need the minigames (A3) and are left out.
3. **A request is only drawn for what the creature can do now:** `feed` names a food on its menu except the treat (two treats raise the sickness chance, `tuning.sickness`; a pellet is allowed: hunger limits it to about one meal every 3 awake hours, and the measured day (decision 8) counts it), `job` a spot its stage opens, `fight` needs a kid or older, `field` any open field, `buy` an item that is not owned and that the creature can pay for: each material in its price is in the bag in the amount needed or is dropped by a spot the creature's stage opens (a baby, a kid and the special gather only Fiber and Herbs). No two open requests have the same type and target. A `buy` request never names the item another request is about to pay. Open requests and waiting slots are never more than 3 together.
4. **Progress belongs to the request,** counted from the moment it is drawn (`have` starts at 0). Meals, minutes, jobs and wins are counted by the event; a purchase is a state check once a minute, below.
5. **A purchase counts once it can no longer be undone.** A `buy` request is done when the item is owned and it is not the purchase still inside its undo window: `lastBuy` is not that item, or more than 10 seconds have passed since it (`now - at > window`). A purchase dated after the current game time (a clock set back) does not count until the clock has passed its 10 seconds, so the window can never reopen on a counted purchase. It is checked every simulated minute, so the request completes 10 to 70 seconds after the buy, and the card says so. Counting at the buy would let "buy, undo, repeat" earn the reward for nothing.
6. **Draws use no saved random state.** A draw seeds its own generator from the state of the simulation's generator at that minute (read with `state()`, never advanced: inside `simulateTo` that local generator is the live one, `save.rngState` is only written back at the end of the call) and the number of draws so far, and writes neither back, so a profile in Kid Mode (or with the board off) replays exactly as before and the same save and time give the same board however the minutes are split. Only the simulation draws; the actions only count. The generator's state depends on what the player did before (a meal, a fight, an egg tap move it), so the board is a function of the whole history; that is accepted (no one can choose what a draw gives).
7. **The slots.** Open requests plus waiting slots are at most 3 together. Each finished request leaves a waiting slot (`doneAt`, what it paid) until `doneAt + 15 minutes`; then one request is drawn for it. A missing slot (a new Adult Mode profile, a save with fewer than 3, a request dropped for not fitting) is drawn at the next simulated minute. Nothing is drawn with no living creature (egg or dead): the slots wait. Death and retiring change nothing on the board: its requests wait for the next creature and are checked against it at the first minute after it hatches (decision 10).
8. **Rewards, `[tune]`:** coins feed 2, field 3, job 3, fight 4, buy 5, read from `data/requests.ts` by type when paid and never stored in the save (a saved number could be hostile); every fourth request drawn also carries an item: the 4th, 8th, 12th and so on, that is the draws numbered 3, 7, 11 from 0, `drawn` read before the draw adds 1. The item is chosen at the draw from the items the player does not own and no open `buy` request names (none left: no item). A reward item that the player owns by the time it is paid is not paid twice (only the coins). The oldest open request that has been open 3 days or more (game time; a clock jump of 3 days gives it too, accepted) pays 25% more coins, rounded up (never zero, never a penalty); two of the same age: the lower id. The numbers are checked against a measured day (`tools/verify/income.ts` extended): with the board, the three-visit player should earn about 1.5 to 2 times the coins of the same player without it (the spec's "2 to 3 days of request and job income" for a useful item); if not, the five numbers change.
9. **A request is paid when it is done,** no Collect button; the slot then shows "Done, +3 coins, next in 12 min" until the replacement. A note over the room says so (Adult Mode's own note, `Game.say`); the coin chip in the strip already ticks.
10. **The board is re-fitted every minute.** Each simulated minute with Adult Mode on, an open request the creature cannot do is dropped (its progress is lost, it was out of reach) and drawn again as a missing slot: a field food it has not got (a new baby, or the special, which keeps its kid stage and loses the field foods), a spot or a fight its stage does not open, a `buy` it can no longer pay for. Every other request, with its progress, stays. Growing up never drops anything except into the special (menus and stages otherwise only widen). The same check cleans a saved board that names something the creature cannot do. With Adult Mode off the board is not touched: the unfit ones are dropped at the first minute after it is turned on.
11. **The board sleeps with Adult Mode.** With the setting off nothing is counted and nothing is drawn; the requests wait as they were. Turning it on draws the missing ones.
12. **Left out:** the spec's training, pickup and bond requests (A3), chained requests, a HUD card or a badge for the board (the header is already tall; offered below), a "next request" line in the context line, notifications. `isUntouched` is not changed: a save that has had a request drawn has had a living creature, so it already has more than one log line.

### Saved state (one field, version stays 3)

```ts
interface Request { id: number; type: "feed" | "field" | "job" | "fight" | "buy"; target: string | null; have: number; item: string | null; drewAt: number }
interface Wait { doneAt: number; coins: number; item: string | null }   // what the finished request paid, for the card only
Save.board: { open: Request[]; waits: Wait[]; drawn: number; done: number }
```

`target` is a food id, a field id, a spot id or an item id (`null` for `fight`). `id` is the draw number. What a request needs (2 meals, 20 minutes, 1) and what it pays are not saved: they come from `data/requests.ts` by type, so a hostile or old number cannot change a payment. `drawn` counts every draw (it drives the item rhythm and seeds the draw), `done` counts every payment (the note over the room compares it). `migrateSave` keeps a request only if its type is known; its `target` is a valid id of that type's own kind (feed: a food id other than the treat, field: an open field, job: a spot, buy: an item, fight: `null`; own-key checks, so `constructor` and `__proto__` are not ids); `id` is a whole number from 0; `have` is a finite number clamped to 0 up to the type's need; `drewAt` is finite; `item` is an item id or `null`. A wait is kept only if `doneAt` is finite, `coins` is a whole number clamped to 0..1000 and `item` is an item id or `null`. A later open request with the same `id` or the same type and target as an earlier one is dropped (one meal must never pay two slots). At most 3 open requests are kept and at most `3 - open` waits; a bad `drawn` or `done` becomes 0, and `drawn` is raised to the highest open `id` + 1 when that is more. (A field food named for a baby loads and is dropped by the first minute's fit check.) An older app version drops the field when it saves (the same limit as `owned`, known-issues.md).

### Edge cases (a test for each; "(b)" is a browser check)

Drawing
1. A living creature in Adult Mode with an empty board gets 3 requests at the first simulated minute and not before; the same save in Kid Mode, an egg or a dead creature gets none; a draw advances nothing of the simulation's generator, so everything else in the save is deep-equal with and without the board (compared with the `board` field left out: `main`'s save has none), and a day of Kid Mode play gives the same result as on `main`.
2. Every request drawn fits the creature, over every form (all 65) and many seeds: no field food for a baby or the special, never the treat, no Trees, Pond or Rock pile below their stage, no fight for a baby, no `buy` of an item whose materials the creature's stage cannot gather and the bag lacks; literal pools per stage (baby, kid and the special gather Fiber and Herbs; baby: pellet, 3 fields, 2 spots, items payable with Fiber, Herbs and coins; kid: also its 3 field foods and a fight; young: also Trees and Pond; adult: also Rock pile; the bag widens the `buy` pool).
3. No two open requests share a type and target; a `buy` names no owned item and no item another request carries as its reward; with every item owned there are no `buy` requests and no reward item; a `field` request is always drawable (3 fields, at most 2 other open requests), so a slot is never left empty (the pools are tested as functions).
4. Draw number 3, 7, 11 (`drawn % 4 === 3`) carry an item (unowned, chosen at the draw), the others none; the card shows it before the request starts, and the item paid is the item shown.
5. The same save and the same time give the same board when run as one step of N minutes, as N steps of one minute, and through a JSON save and load in the middle.
6. A waiting slot is drawn exactly 15 minutes after `doneAt` (one minute earlier nothing), each slot on its own clock; the new request's `drewAt` is that minute.

Counting
7. A meal of the named food counts once when the result is "ate" (a dish meal counts as the food it is a meal of); a refused meal (full, asleep, away, off the menu) counts nothing; another food counts nothing; meals eaten before the draw count nothing; the second meal completes it and a third does not count again.
8. A minute at the named field counts when the creature is awake, at that place and not on a job; at home, in another field, asleep or on a job count nothing; 20 minutes complete it. A minute is credited to the place the creature is in when the minute is simulated (the same rounding as the baby's field count: up to 59 seconds at a change of place), so the same 25 minutes spent in one place while the app was open and while it was closed give the same board.
9. A finished job at the named spot counts, also one that finished while the app was closed; a call back early counts nothing; another spot counts nothing.
10. A won fight counts; a lost one, and one run away from, count nothing.
11. A `buy` request is done when the item is owned and not undoable: bought and left, it completes at the first minute after the 10 second window; bought and undone inside the window it never counts and the request stays open; a purchase made later replaces `lastBuy`, so the earlier one counts at the next minute; buy, undo, buy, undo, repeated, pays nothing; a purchase dated after the game time (a clock set back) does not count; buy, set the clock back 61 minutes, step a minute, set it forward to 5 seconds after the purchase and undo: the undo refunds and the request has not been paid, and when the window then passes the request is not paid for an item the player no longer owns.
12. With Adult Mode off nothing counts and nothing is drawn, and turning it on again goes on from where it was.

Paying
13. A finished request pays its coins (plus 25% rounded up if it is the oldest and open 3 days or more: 2 days 23 hours 59 minutes pays plain, exactly 3 days pays the bonus, a younger one never does, two of the same age give it to the lower id), pays its item if the player still does not own it (otherwise only coins, and `owned` never holds an item twice), moves its slot to a wait with what was paid, adds a line to the log and adds 1 to `done`.
14. Two requests finished by one minute (two `buy` requests) are both paid; one event never completes a request of another type or target.
15. Rewards do not change the wall (a wallpaper paid is owned and not hung), are not undoable, and a bought-then-paid item cannot be bought again (`buy` refuses "owned", as before).

Time
16. The clamps: a `doneAt` or `drewAt` after the current game time (a clock set back) is brought back to it, so a refill is never more than 15 minutes away and an age never negative; a clock set forward a year draws what is due, once per slot; a `doneAt`, `drewAt` or `coins` that is `NaN` or infinite never reaches the board (migrate drops or clamps it).
17. ### What it is

Four hats join the Shop as items 10 to 13 (thirteen in all). A bought hat is in `owned`; the player puts one on from its card ("Wear", then "Take off") and the creature wears it in the room, the home and the habitat. A hat does nothing to care, growth, combat or income. It is drawn onto the creature's own picture by one rule that reads only the picture, so it works for all 65 forms in every pose.

### Checked in the code (2026-11-25)

- Every picture of a creature comes from `faceSprite(form, kind, frame)` in `art/face.ts` (a cached `Sprite`: `rows` and `palette`). `kind` is one of 13 (standing: open, closed, half, up, happy, yawn; slumped: droop, droopSad, droopClosed, droopYawn, droopHappy; lying: lie0, lie1). The slumped and lying pictures are wider than the form's grid (`lieDown`), and every picture is a pixel grid, so a rule on the finished grid needs no pose logic.
- The player's creature is drawn in two places that matter: `art/scene.ts` (one function: the home, the old one-line room and the field picture a creature stands in, `worldOf` in `Stage.tsx`) and `art/habitat.ts` (`drawCreature`). The arena (`art/arena.ts`) and the hatch and evolve scenes (`art/moment.ts`) draw it too. ### The rule (one rule, no per-form data)

1. **Crown row:** the first row of the finished picture whose filled span (first to last filled cell) is at least `max(3, round(0.4 * W))` cells wide, where `W` is the widest filled span of any row. A picture with no filled cell gets no hat.
2. **Centre:** the middle of that row's span; the hat's left column is `Math.floor(centre - hatWidth / 2 + 0.5)` (round half up, written this way so that the Python mock, whose `round` is half to even, uses the same rule).
3. **Height:** the hat's last row lies on the crown row (it replaces those cells where the hat has a cell). If that would put the hat's first row above row 0, the hat moves down until its first row is row 0 (it then covers more of the head).
4. **Edges:** a hat cell outside the picture (left, right, or below its last row) is dropped, never wrapped. The picture keeps its size.
5. **Size:** a small hat for baby and kid (7 wide), a large one for young and adult (13 wide), chosen by the form's stage. The grids are stored finished (outline included, the bottom row has no outline because it sits on the head).
6. **Colours:** a hat has its own letters, the digits 1 (outline) to 5, in the picture's palette (copied, never changed in place), so no form's letter is ever replaced.
7. The hatless picture stays in the cache untouched: a hat is made from a copy.
8. **No jump between a form's two standing drawings (found on the real code, 2026-11-25):** for the second drawing the crown row and the centre are held within 2 rows and 2 columns of where the rule puts them on the first drawing. Without this one form (droopstar, whose second drawing lifts a claw above the head) carried the hat 10 rows up and 5 columns across every time it swapped drawings while walking; the other 64 forms move at most 2. Generic, no per-form data.
